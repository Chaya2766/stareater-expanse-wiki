Felicity Techtree: Ion Beam Printing

Ion Beam Printing is the process enabling production of all the computing components in the Felicity tech tree. The dedicated piece of equipment for this is the SPIP-V3 printer capable of producing all of its own small parts, and among other things, civic-2 chips, at a great cost of time.

Here is a math document regarding some of the exact numbers mentioned here.

The self-producable ion printer is its own separate open source project which Felicity included in her mining settlement's official technology collection. The third iteration of the design is a complete rework, rebuilding the system from the ground up to avoid the failures of version 1 and 2. Version 3 had already been finalized and is receiving no more updates, allowing the community to fully utilize it without worrying about loss of compatibility, but at the time that Felicity incorporated it into her technology ecosystem there had already been multiple years of ongoing work on version 4 and discussions of potentially needing another total redesign due to technical limitations not easily explained to casual hobbyists.

A 3d-printer that can print every single one of its own parts had been a dream of many people since even before the first 3d printer had been practically realized. The SPIP is a 25th century evolution of that dream, denoting a small but decisive step towards a general purpose replicator like those theorized in ancient science fiction.

This machine can lay down material at as much as 5 micromol per second, at which point heat dissipation becomes the limiting factor, but for complex nanoscopic structures where perfect atomic precision is required, such as transistors and internal traces of a processor, the deposition rate must be much lower, on the order of 5 to 10 nanomol per second. Version 3 was also the first in the SPIP project to reach the stated goal of printing a complete, usable civic-2 chip within less than a megasecond.
