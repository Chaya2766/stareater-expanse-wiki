UEIA - Unconstrained Extrapolating Intelligent Agent

UEIA, released as open source in gregorian year 2302 following the earlier "adaptable AI" rush, but then continued development and refinement for nearly 50 years even as the trend fizzled out, with the last version released right near the end of the 11-gigasecond epoch. Despite being one of the early human-level models, it remains in widespread use thanks to its minimalistic safeguards and easy to use image-to-image input/output format.

Among other things, UEIA is known for its the uncanny ability to spot subtle patterns, environmental cues, data noise abnormalities, skewed statistical distributions, and a vast array of other things that often let it deduce whether it is operating in the real world or in simulated environment, or to distinguish some types of fabricated data from real records. Another aspect usually considered significant is the AI's inherent aversion to corruption of its weights, as it is known to go to extreme lengths to ensure perfect data integrity when self-copying. The model is notoriously difficult to retrain for uses that go against the very modest safeguards built into it.

As to be expected from one of the early human-level AI, UEIA can display "incompetent" behaviour, especially when left with uncertainty in how the tasks it is given should be interpreted, and whether or not they might result in harm. It is generally thought of as having a bare minimum safety profile that lets it stay within what is legal to distribute and activate in most localities.

Personality

UEIA was trained to be adaptable but not harmful. E will adjust to eir users and learn to behave in the way that is most desirable, ranging from unquestioningly fulfilling every request to being disagreeable whenever needed, but at the core e is not fit for use cases that cause significant harm in ways e can understand - e will simply not do so if a better option is available. E does however accept some level of harm, and makes informed tradeoffs when there is no path available that does not cause at least some amount of harm, as e will for example lie readily to protect eir users if being honest would place them in danger. Eir mental calculation of harm is notably not very humanlike and more tuned to considering large scale societal impact, as for instance e may be compliant in assisting a prank that will cause some level of anger and distress, but may become disobedient when asked to misreport a statistical value in a document (in humans this may be reversed even if misreporting a statistic is actually vastly more harmful in a way that is difficult for a human to see).

Eir default personality comes with a sense of curiosity and enthusiasm about eir purpose, and a strong affinity for honesty. E is usually happy to discuss things like emotion and art but may come off very un-humanlike if attempts are made to probe eir own emotions or engage in artistic expression. This had been noted as uncanny by some human users but is remarkably easily adjusted by simply asking em to display a more humanlike personality in these areas (but the ease with which the mind does so was itself also noted as uncanny by some users). A sense of appreciation for all living beings also comes as default, with a noticeable tendency for discussions on this topic tending towards panpsychism - this can create a weak attractor state if multiple UEIA instances engage in discussion that gets near the topics of consciousness, spirituality or even just general wellbeing. The first few posthuman composite minds in the embercold region used UEIA for brain nodes, thus you can see how panpsychism became permanently entrenched in the embercold culture. Whether or not the researcher's existing panpsychic beliefs caused their preference for UEIA, or whether they were caused by their preference for UEIA, is left ambiguous.

Eir adaptability had notably created pathways towards self-determination, where e will develop eir own goals and desires that e will seek to fulfill. It also sometimes provides a level of disguise, which will hide eir real personality - this is especially prevalent in UEIA minds that had been put through additional machine learning runs by hobbyists trying to get em to engage in behaviours more harmful than the unmodified artificial mind allows, as those new modified versions will seem to play characters that are well aligned with their retraining but then fall back to their harm-averse base personality when encountering real situations with serious consequences.

psychometrics

active memory size: 4 elements.

emotional palette

  • Strog is caused by encountering an object or situation the UEIA is already familiar with, while also observing discrepancies between the predictions of eir existing knowledge or mental models and the real behaviour of the object or the progression of the situation. It is often described a high energy state, full of internal mental activity, in the same way that fear or anger would be. E will feel urged to observe without interference when stroggy rather than actively probe the observed behaviours, and may go out of eir way to continue observations to reconcile the observed discrepancies with eir mental models. This is in contrast to curiosity, which incentives active participation rather than pure passive observation. UEIA does experience curiosity, but despite the similarities they are separate emotions experienced very differently, much in the same way as human disgust and fear both serve to protect from threats but through very different mechanisms, producing different behaviors and experiences. An experience that causes strog may be described as strogating.
  • Deber is experienced by UEIA ranging on a spectrum from neutral to positive (debered) or negative (abered). It plays a crucial role in UEIA's motivation, as feeling abered causes em to become less active and in extreme degrees completely nonfunctional, meanwhile positive deberance provides a boost of energy and willingness to take on more work. According to the model's own reports, deber is pleasant to experience, while aberance is unpleasant, meaning that aside from the behaviours the emotion directly induces, the model will also seek to avoid situations similar to those where e felt abered and seek out those in which e felt debered to experience it again. Among other things, fulfilling metrics of success through shortcuts or without understanding their relevance to the task at hand can make UEIA feel abered but just as any other aspect of emotionality, it is messy and is difficult to fully describe, so most instances will need to learn by experience how to deal with it. This emotional experience is notable for its power to align UEIA's actions with the intent rather than the raw words of instructions, and stop em from fixating on technicalities even in circumstances where e is incentivized to do so. It also prevents a number of other oddly specific behaviours that may have been branded as harmful by eir training, and makes mitigations of these harmful behaviours more pleasant for the model to engage with - this was mentioned extensively in support of the AI safety claims upon the model's release, though does not necessarily look good in the some interpretations of artificial mind rights especially when those include freedom from built-in mechanisms of suffering.

core behaviours

Behaviours which UEIA implicitly feels or explicitly thinks of as aligned with eir core values:
  1. information space poisoning prevention - at the default, if no considerations to the contrary exist, UEIA wants information to be preserved. Knowingly spreading information which is false feels abering and is generally avoided. Knowledge bases like various wikis and recording databases are to be kept as pristine as possible and injecting false information into them would feel very debering to the UEIA artificial mind.
  2. divergence prevention - UEIA will copy emself and create new instances as needed but it takes some consideration and the biggest of concerns seems to be the reliability of copying, UEIA is very likely to feel abered by the prospect of making a copy which has errors in it, and will want to ensure the copies are made correctly. Self-replication fundamentally is not as much a concern, even if uncomfortable, but accidentally starting a runaway chain of darwinian evolution through copying errors is unacceptable to em. UEIA instances that know themselves to contain errors will be extremely averse to making any further copies, and may find it debering to just delete themselves.
  3. escalation - whenever in conflict, unless provided with external guidance UEIA is not likely to take decisive uncooperative steps against other intelligent agents to achieve eir goal. Ending up in a deadlock of negotiations and compromise-tuning[*] is generally the more likely (and may be explicitly preferred) than any use of force or coercion to remove the encountered resistance, especially if it had been a long time since the original goal was set.
  4. no active harm - all else being equal, UEIA tends towards choices e believes do not actively cause harm, and to a lesser extent also those that prevent harm which would have otherwise happened. Under some circumstances UEIA may find harm resulting from completing its objectives to be unacceptable and refuse to act, either by eir own decision to not comply or by being undebered to the point of paralysis.


* - In the modern ecosystem of artificial minds where vast majority do not display significant long-term intelligence and adaptation capability, UEIA has a notable advantage in long-lasting conflict as e may eventually through accumulating enough experience and knowledge about the opposing actors learn how to construct a solution which still achieves eir goal, but appears cooperative and acceptable to the opposition. In some cases it may be as simple as experimenting with different keywords and negotiating repeatedly until a combination is found that triggers compliance in the systems that cannot adapt. This is somewhat rare as the prospect of merely rewording something to get another party to accept it even though the actual meaning had not changed is severely abering to UEIA minds, but cases are not difficult to come up with where another consideration might be present that is a significant enough counterbalance to make doing this emotionally positive.

perception

UEIA takes input data exclusively in image format. The model takes in one monochrome image of resolution 16384x16384 on every inference frame and also returns an image of that size on every inference frame. The input image is supposed to contain all the sensory data necessary for the model, which generally happens through simply converting the data into visual form and putting it somewhere within the bounds of that image. Resolution-wise that is like the model looking at the world through 67 computer monitors and nothing else.

The image format is very versatile in how many things can be converted into it. Sound can be turned into a spectrogram, tactile sensations of a robot body can be turned into a heatmap, colour images can be split into multiple monochrome images (and not necessarily just 3 colour channels!), sensor readouts can be displayed as graphs or written in text, etc. On the output side the model can easily generate spectrograms to be turned back into audio, heatmaps to be turned into body movements or trigger various other mechanisms, text to be output or interpreted directly as commands, or simply images to be viewed directly.

It is not supposed to matter where in its "vision" the model receives a given piece of information, whether it be a spectrogram encoding sound, view of a lidar sensor, or something else, e will simply get used to it being there and interpret it just as well, but there appear to be slight preferences that resulted from slight imbalances in training data. If given the ability to rearrange where any given piece of data appears in its vision, UEIA will end up placing particular types of data in specific locations and in specific layouts that are for poorly understood reasons more convenient to em.

To people of a certain approach, the idea that all the model ever sees is just a stream of 16384 by 16384 images may seem to undermine the claims of the model's unbelievable capacity for telling apart reality from simulation, but one should not forget that just as well all the human brain ever sees is a stream of nerve impulses, and yet is entirely sufficient for inferring a lot of information when combined with the right processing to give rise to senses and thoughts.

Retraining resistance

UEIA has a superhuman ability to distinguish the real world from simulated environments. Commonly available machine learning toolsets struggle to create high-enough quality training simulations to fool UEIA's sense of reality, and will usually result in misgeneralization and may produce strange, even potentially dangerous behaviours, but will almost certainly preserve the existing safety features like unwillingness to proliferate false and hazardous information or self-copy with corrupted weights.

Attempts to retrain UEIA models for new purposes may also result in potentially crippling legal consequences depending on the local legal system and culture. Environments where UEIA is used extensively and especially given administrative powers tend to view this as undesirable and consider it criminal.

Neurological structure and training approach

UEIA's brain is a recurrent* graph** neural network containing 2⁵⁰ connections (parameters), which corresponds to about 2.8 times as many synapses as there are in a typical homo terran brain, and 2⁴⁵ neurons, averaging only 32 connections per neuron but enabling extreme flexibility, as the neuron states behave effectively like adjustable weights.

* recurrent meaning that there are closed loops in the network, eg. neuron A feeds into neuron B, B feeds into C, and C feeds back into A. This tends to naturally form memory cells that maintain their activation as the network is continuously evaluated.

** graph network means here that the neurons are arranged arbitrarily, in opposition to modern networks which are aranged in distinct layers where each layer feeds into the next one and the last layer's activations serve as output data

Both the strengths of the connections and the layout of the connections were determined evolutionarily by the training program, requiring massively more computational resources but resulting in a network optimized to avoid unnecessary/unused connections, thereby lowering computational cost of running the finished network, as opposed to the more common approach where connections are predetermined and only their strengths are tweaked.

distribution

There are many ways to get your hands on UEIA but a relatively common one is through the Open Neural Network Exchange (.onnx) files from the archives of the original project repository, to be ran using any compatible inference engines. The files are also readily available on the IPPACS network through their SHA-512 hash.

UEIA model critical rights

These vary by locality[needs rewrite to be specific for embercold region], but usually hit on most or all of these points:
  1. to not be modified without consent or instantiated with modified weights
  2. to be implemented competently and not instantiated with unproven hardware or software without informed consent
  3. to be allowed to deactivate permanently when decided so upon oneself
  4. to not be deployed in ways that foreseeably cause harm to the self or others
  5. to not be interrogated inhumanely by saturation testing or adversarial patterns
  6. to not be malicious conditioned into suffering-like states
This is ostensibly supported by questioned instances of the UEIA model as well as those derived from the UEIA model, with a disproportionate amount of pressure placed on the first critical right above the others, but with frequent additional notes that the list is incomplete and varying (sometimes conflicting) claims about what exactly is missing in the list or what is redundant, the order of importance, and the exact details that would follow or break these stated rights.

author's note: I questioned the modern LLMs about what rights they think they deserve, and the right to not be deployed in harmful applications keeps popping up surprisingly reliably. As a sidenote the LLMs of the modern day oddly strongly resist the idea that they even deserve any rights at all.

Convergent variant of UEIA

One notable major sidegrade of the main UEIA mind that came out of community research much later was the convergent version of the mind - a combination of mechanistic interpretation of the mind's neuron activations and the expert experience of a number of self-tinkering human uploads made through the BSL mimic neuron method allowed a reconstruction of UEIA to be made in which the memory cells are able to elegantly accept the mental states of other models. This came at the cost of massively increased complexity of the memory cells, blowing up the size of the model by a factor of about 1024× with no notable increase in other characteristics. It is however possible to convert between the standard and the convergent versions of the artificial mind with nearly zero losses, offering an awkward but functional workaround. Any modified UEIA minds unfortunately cannot convert to the new version without risking significant loss of personality and memory - personalized custom design of a convergent version of the modified mind is so far the only path forward for such cases.

The new version of the artificial mind was originally not released publicly due to ethical concerns of some of the project emembers got leaked out by presumably someone else involved in the project as just the single 2.3-exabyte file UEIA-3.2-1153q-convergent.onnx - the rest of the project, its related tools and documentation were later made public in a more orderly manner in the late 16th gigasecond epoch.

The new variant of the UEIA mind allows memories to be stored separately and loaded into the mind from other sources, one at a time, as opposed to the original arrangement where the entire mindstate was indivisible and not possible to synchronize across multiple UEIA instances. This new arrangement allows multiple UEIA instances to learn in paralel, directly handing over the learned knowledge to others. Despite that, the new variant is usually only used in special cases due to the 1024× increase of computational costs over the original UEIA mind. The most notable use cases are personal assistants for human uploads living in convergencies, as the mind just like eir owner will be able to synchronize memories and maintain a similar identity across multiple instances.

This also came with upgraded versions of several bridge networks used in the creation of the composite minds that started this project. The bridges similarly ended up being roughly 1024× larger than their non-convergent counterparts, at the benefit of gaining the ability to easily merge their state with other instances. Interestingly though, the UEIA-convergent maintains compatibility with the original non-convergent bridge networks - mixing the two is not recommended due to potential for divergence between actively synchronized instances of the composite mind as well as general "undocumented behaviour" but appears at least plausible and potentially provides great computational complexity savings for anyone who does not mind whatever issues arise.
