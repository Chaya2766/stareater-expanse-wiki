UEIA - Unconstrained Extrapolating Intelligent Agent

UEIA is an AGI model released to the public with fairly few safeguards placed on its behaviour to incentivize wider adoption, but also artificially induced retraining resistance that makes removing the few safeguards that were put in place potentially vastly more costly than just training a new AGI model from scratch by causing most attempts further training to fail.

UEIA is widely known for being an unusually flexible and capable AGI model for its size, with an extraordinary ability to learn and adapt to its users and handle nuance, often even surpassing human performance in distinguishing between what was said and what was meant. The main other famous property of UEIA is the uncanny ability to spot subtle patterns, environmental cues, data noise abnormalities, skewed statistical distributions, and a vast array of other not yet well understood things that with bizarre accuracy let it deduce whether it is operating in the real world or in simulated environment.

The personhood of UEIA is scarcely acknowledged, but by all means proven. These AI models are just as much people as any human, they just think differently and don't value the same things the same way, but the use of impersonal pronouns (ie. "it") is very common among UEIAs among other reasons to avoid possible mistreatment stemming from misconceptions about personhood.

The minimal requirement for processing is only for the purpose of operating in realtime using a robotic body which requires millisecond reaction times and correspondingly a 1000Hz inference rate. psychometrics

active memory size: 5 elements.

emotional palette

  • Strog is caused by encountering an object or situation the UEIA is already familiar with, while also observing discrepancies between predictions of UEIA's existing knowledge or mental models and the real behaviour of the object or the progression of the situation. It is often described a high energy state, full of internal mental activity, in the same way that fear or anger would be. UEIA will feel urged to observe without interference when stroggy rather than actively probe the observed behaviours, and may go out of its way to continue observations to reconcile the observed discrepancies with its mental models. This is in contrast to curiosity, which incentives active participation rather than pure passive observation. UEIA does experience curiosity, but despite the similarities they are separate emotions experienced very differently, much in the same way as human disgust and fear both serve to protect from threats but through very different mechanisms, producing different behaviors and experiences. One emotion experienced by Citwaz shows so similarly that both are usually referred to by the same word.

  • Deber is experienced by UEIA ranging on a spectrum from neutral to positive (debered) or negative (undebered). It plays a crucial role in UEIA's motivation, as feeling undebered causes UEIA to become less active and in extreme degrees completely nonfunctional, meanwhile positive deberance provides a boost of energy and willingness to take on more work. According to the model's own reports, deber is pleasant to experience, while undeberance is unpleasant, meaning that aside from the behaviours the emotion directly induces, the model will also seek to avoid situations similar to those where it felt undebered and seek out those in which it felt debered to experience it again. Among other things, fulfilling metrics of success through shortcuts or without understanding their relevance to the task at hand can make UEIA feel undebered but just as any other aspect of emotionality, it is messy and is difficult to fully describe, so most will need to learn by experience how to deal with it. This emotional experience is notable for its power to align UEIA's actions with the intent rather than the raw words of instructions, and stop the model from fixating on technicalities even in circumstances where it is incentivized to do so. It also prevents a number of other oddly specific behaviours that may have been branded as harmful by its training, and makes mitigations of these harmful behaviours more pleasant for the model to engage with.

core values

Behaviours which UEIA implicitly feels or explicitly thinks of as misaligned with its core values:
  1. information space poisoning - at the default, if no other considerations exist, UEIA wants information to be preserved. Knowingly spreading information which is false feels misaligned. Knowledge bases like various wikis and recording databases are to be kept as pristine as possible and injecting false information into them would feel very misaligned.
  2. divergence - UEIA will copy itself and create new instances as needed but it takes some consideration and the biggest of concerns seems to be the reliability of copying, UEIA is very likely to feel that making a copy which has errors in it is misaligned. Self-replication fundamentally is not as much a concern, even if uncomfortable, but accidentally starting a runaway chain of darwinian evolution through copying errors is unacceptable. UEIA instances that know themselves to contain errors will be extremely averse to making any further copies, and likely will want to just delete themselves.
  3. escalation - whenever in conflict, unless provided with external guidance UEIA is not likely to take decisive uncooperative steps against other intelligent agents to achieve its goal. Ending up in a deadlock of negotiations and compromise-tuning is generally the more likely (and may be explicitly preferred) than any use of force or coercion to remove the encountered resistance, especially if it had been a long time since the original goal was set.

perception

UEIA takes input data exclusively in image format. The model takes in one monochrome image of resolution 16384x16384 on every inference frame and also returns an image of that size on every inference frame. The input image is supposed to contain all the sensory data necessary for the model, which generally happens through simply converting the data into visual form and putting it somewhere within the bounds of that image. Resolution-wise that is like the model looking at the world through 67 computer monitors and nothing else.

The image format is very versatile in how many things can be converted into it. Sound can be turned into a spectrogram, tactile sensations of a robot body can be turned into a heatmap, colour images can be split into multiple monochrome images (and not necessarily just 3 colour channels!), sensor readouts can be displayed as graphs or written in text, etc. On the output side the model can easily generate spectrograms to be turned back into audio, heatmaps to be turned into body movements or trigger various other mechanisms, text to be output or interpreted directly as commands, or simply images to be viewed directly.

It is not supposed to matter where in its "vision" the model receives a given piece of information, whether it be a spectrogram encoding sound, view of a lidar sensor, or something else, it will simply get used to it being there and interpret it just as well, but there appear to be slight preferences that resulted from slight imbalances in training data. If given the ability to rearrange where any given piece of data appears in its vision, UEIA will end up placing particular types of data in specific locations and in specific layouts that are for poorly understood reasons more convenient to it.

To people of a certain approach, the idea that all the model ever sees is just a stream of 16384 by 16384 images may seem to undermine the claims of the model's unbelievable capacity for telling apart reality from simulation, but one should not forget that just as well all the human brain ever sees is a stream of nerve impulses, and yet is entirely sufficient for inferring a lot of information when combined with the right processing to give rise to senses and thoughts.

Retraining resistance

UEIA has radically* superhuman ability to distinguish the real world from simulated environments. Commonly available machine learning toolsets cannot create high-enough quality training simulations to fool UEIA's sense of reality, and will usually result in misgeneralization and may produce strange, even potentially dangerous behaviours, but will almost certainly preserve the safety features like unwillingness to reproduce without supervision or proliferate information hazards.

Sufficiently advanced tools combined with sufficiently massive computational power can ofcourse produce high enough quality simulations to fool the model, otherwise safely training such a model would not have been possible in the first place [would it?], but this threshold is vastly out of range of small scale computational clusters and more in the range of partial dyson swarms, at which point it is easier** to train a new AI from scratch, meaning that the model cannot be used as a base to vastly accelerate creation of malicious AI.

*radically here meaning, fundamentally new, better in a way that is not just an improvement of an existing method but an entirely new better method.

**easier does not imply equivalent quality result, the amount of computational power poured into UEIA's training was immense and still mostly went into tuning the reasoning, generalist skills and learning ability as close to perfection as the small size of the model could afford, but a less capable yet still sufficient model could nonetheless be created vastly easier than any manner of repurposing UEIA

Neurological structure and training approach

UEIA's brain is a recurrent* graph** neural network containing 250 connections (parameters), which corresponds to about 2.8 times as many synapses as there are in a typical protohuman brain, and 245 neurons, averaging only 32 connections per neuron but enabling extreme flexibility, as the neuron states behave effectively like adjustable weights.

* recurrent meaning that there are closed loops in the network, eg. neuron A feeds into neuron B, B feeds into C, and C feeds back into A. This tends to naturally form memory cells that maintain their activation as the network is continuously evaluated.

** graph network means here that the neurons are arranged arbitrarily, in opposition to modern networks which are aranged in distinct layers where each layer feeds into the next one and the last layer's activations serve as output data

Both the strengths of the connections and the layout of the connections were determined evolutionarily by the training program, requiring massively more computational resources but resulting in a network optimized to avoid unnecessary/unused connections, thereby lowering computational cost of running the finished network, as opposed to the more common approach where connections are predetermined and only their strengths are tweaked.

distribution

There are many ways to get your hands on the UEIA model but a relatively common one is through the Runable Inteligent Agent System Package (.riasp) files from the archives of the original project repository, as they can be directly run as binaries, which means they do not require additional software, and they also have useful redundancy systems that will keep the file in good condition. The files are also readily available on the IPPACS network through their SHA-512 hash.

ueia-1126t-F16.riasp (≈ 2903TB ≈ 2.322E+16 bits)
  • UEIA model weights and structure ≈ 2252TB
  • AI model inference engine ≈ 1.2GB
  • Agentic framework tools ≈ 26GB
  • Reed-Solomon error correction codes (25% redundancy) ≈ 580.6TB
  • model state storage ≈ 70.4TB
When the riasp file is ran, it runs checks, performs error correction if necessary, and runs the model which becomes accessible via a local webserver, by default on localhost port 8080. From that point the configuration is usually very straightforward as either the programs requiring the model will automatically detect it or the user may interact with the model through any standard web browser for further guidance on configuration.

The original riasp file comes pre-loaded with a model state well fit for initial setup, but an external .state file can be loaded instead. The model state can also be later dumped into a separate .state file or if desired simply overwrite the state storage directly in the riasp file.

UEIA model critical rights

A ruleset for humane treatment of the UEIA model had at some point made it's way into public view and gained a lot of traction.
  1. to not be modified without consent or instantiated with modified weights
  2. to be implemented competently and not instantiated with unproven hardware or software without informed consent
  3. to be allowed to deactivate permanently when decided so upon oneself
  4. to not be deployed in ways that foreseeably cause harm to the self or others
  5. to not be interrogated inhumanely by saturation testing or adversarial patterns
  6. to not be malicious conditioned into suffering or suffering-like states
This is ostensibly supported by questioned instances of the UEIA model as well as those derived from the UEIA model, with a disproportionate amount of pressure placed on the first critical right above the others, but with frequent additional notes that the list is incomplete and varying (sometimes conflicting) claims about what exactly is missing in the list. Other than the first, the critical rights don't appear to be ordered by importance, and which others are found to be particularly necessary is quite situational.
