Felicity Techtree: Low Temperature Electrolysis of mixed metal oxides

The LTE process can be thought of as a precursor to a whole big branch of the Felicity tech tree, as it trivializes the extraction of several metals from asteroid material.

Asteroid dust is mixed in with a concentrated solution of sodium hydroxide at close to 100oC and a current is passed through it, reducing the metal oxides from the dust into a mixed alloy of these metals.

Once the process is done, the cathode will have grown a beautiful and heavy tree of crystallized metal that can be lifted out of the machine and scraped off for further processing.

Extracted material

A 100kg batch of asteroid regolith with the breakdown of extracted elements above would be turned into 23.573279kg of Kamacite containing primarily iron but also a lot of other elements, 9.46kg of oxygen gas, as well as 66.966721 kg of an enriched sludge of regolith and sodium hydroxide solution full of elements that can't be extracted with this simple process. Using the machine provided in the tech tree this consumes a total of 348MJ in electrical energy. Once the process is done, the cathode will have grown a beautiful and heavy tree of crystallized metal that can be lifted out of the machine and scraped off for further processing.

Among those elements which are left in the regolith are some very important ones like chromium, which is needed for stainless steel, or lithium, which is needed for high-performance batteries. These lacking elements are the reason that the LTE process can be thought of as a precursor to it's own branch in the tech tree, as the specific set of elements it provides forces a lot of downstream design choices that would have otherwise made no sense.

Low Temperature Electrolysis refinery

The machine consists mainly of a large (60cm × 60cm × 60cm) electrolysis chamber, where the concentrated sodium hydroxide solution is poured, and the fine asteroid regolith is added for processing. The chamber has additional metal plates to increase its radiating area, as it produces a lot of heat during operation. An assembly of 5 carbon electrodes is lowered into the soluton and push current through it, flowing from 4 anodes in the corners to one central cathode. The flow of current from each direction is adjusted as needed to prevent the crystal growth from short circuiting the electrodes, and shuts off the affected cathode in the case that happens.

The machine pulls an average of 4134 watts while in operation, and needs 23.38 hours (84.17 kiloseconds) to complete one batch, assuming that it is filled with the intended 100kg of regolith. Over the course of the process, 348MJ of energy is consumed, and by the end of it the central cathode is overgrown with crystallized kamacite. This crystal growth can be scraped off and sent for further processing, or potentially just used for construction without further refining.

The metal produced contains all the reduced elements as impurities, and is structurally equivalent to wrought iron. Due to the lack of chromium, it is not resistant to corrosion. It is also magnetic.

Using the provided electrolysis machine, kamacite costs 14.7625MJ/kg to produce and can be made at a rate of just over 1.008kg/h.

The exact composition of the extracted kamacite ofcourse depends on the asteroid regolith, but an average expected composition can be given as such: 93.326% Iron, 5.514% Nickel, 0.250% Cobalt, 0.076% Zinc, 0.047% Copper, 0.009% Germanium, 0.006% Selenium, 0.003% Gallium, 0.001% Arsenic, 0.001% Lead, 0.001% Tin, 0.001% Molybdenum, and 0.765% trace elements that fail to round up to 0.001%
