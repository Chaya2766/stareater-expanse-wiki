Home page - Stareater Expanse

Stareater Expanse is my hard sci-fi worldbuilding project that combines various elements of my previous largely abandoned writing, worldbuilding and art projects.

A lot of my writing is heavily influenced by Science and Futurism with Isaac Arthur as well as more recently Orion's Arm, but first and foremost by whatever math and logic I see fit to use as a basis for some fun extrapolation. You will also sometimes find documents that present said math and logic as openly as I could manage to.

The site is hosted from this github repository.

site elements to be aware of:

There are usually cards at the very bottom of the page, which are just illustrated links to other pages.
text underlined with a dashed cyan line will show extra info when you mouse over it
inline tips also occur, mainly in places where no matter what you do with the size of your browser window you wouldn't be able to see the whole popup
Meta content, that is information basing on things not present in the setting or just talking about writing intent, are marked with this colour (this lights up for easier reading when hovered over). This is occasionally useful for immersion reasons and might also make it more clear when I'm writing something that should not be taken as establishing in-lore events.
abandoned content is grayed out, and represents that the given piece of content had once existed but had been mostly written out by now, yet was also judged still valuable enough to preserve in a way that keeps it visible - this is a hint that I had not fully given up on the content but don't expect it to be worked on in the near future
content which I deemed to be of questionable realism, also used for content that relies on information that is expected to be verified in the near future and potentially disproven as a result
external content, used by permission or by a permissive license
work in progress content, in all honesty used very inconsistently - the intended use of this element was for me to mark the content actively being worked on with this so that the readers know to expect that that content might change in the near future, but in reality a vast amount of content is either actively being worked on or planned to be rewritten yet not marked as such. At this point it might be easier to mark the content which I had definitely finished and expect to stay unchanged rather than the content which might still undergo big changes.
