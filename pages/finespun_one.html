Finespun Station One

Finespun Station One is the first of the rotating habitats constructed by Felicity in orbit of the Finespun asteroid.

On the left the station is shown with a section cut away to show the internal structure. The station design is at least locally considered notable for producing pleasantly round acceleration figures for each floor when the runway is calibrated to experience some multiple of 0.5m/s2.

The environment as a whole tends to be uncomfortable for guests as a result of the administrator being an odd kind of person that actually enjoys strong coriolis forces and the oddities of nonstandard atmosphere rather than trying to avoid them, but since there are rarely any guests, the rotation rate of the station is often adjusted to suit the visitors.

Structure

Finespun One is made out of 100mm thick kamacite plating, produced from the local asteroid regolith through low temperature electrolysis and used without much further processing.

Despite being made of a material not resistant to corrosion, it is filled with a low pressure pure-oxygen atmosphere, mainly due to the fact that oxygen is a cheap byproduct of the local kamacite refining, whereas nitrogen-rich air would be expensive and necessitate more sophisticated airlock mechanisms that recover the air instead of venting it into space. The structure inevitably will rust through eventually, but is expected to remain safely usable for at least another century.

The station is divided into 24 sections that retain pressure independently. There are 3 levels to the ring, each 15m tall, with radii of 70m, 55m and 40m, producing apparent gravity of respectively 5.6m/s2, 4.4m/s2 and 3.2m/s2. Each of these levels is also divided into 8 separate rooms along the ring.

The "top" of the ring, which is exposed to space at a radius of 25m and centrifugal acceleration of 2m/s2, might be thought of as a landing zone for small spacecraft, mostly for the mining vessels that collect regolith from Finespun and bring it to the station for refining. It is often referred to as the "runway" because with the low tangential velocity of the hull there, space transport vehicles equipped with wheels can simply drive anti-spinwards at about 7m/s to completely cancel out the centrifugal acceleration, largely removing the need for powerful thrusters.

Internal environment

The internal atmosphere is 21kPa of pure oxygen, which delivers the exact same amount of oxygen as standard terran air at standard terran pressure but with none of the other gasses, most notably nitrogen. Temperature is vaguely kept some safe margin away from freezing as a necessity for the continued functioning of the station, eg. within a few kelvin of 285K ( 12oC / 53oF ). Moisture levels practically never reach 50% or above.

Visitors are generally advised to use nonflamable clothing or request such as needed - or keep their spacesuits on if it is safe and conveniently possible. Any use of fire is discouraged, as despite the identical amount of oxygen as in the common terran environment, the low atmospheric pressure causes flames to be considerably expanded compared to what most people are ready to expect (an example is usually presented of a thin piece of wood burning in the local atmosphere, producing what looks like a hazy aura of orange glow instead of the typical flame with a clear boundary and upwards direction).

Runway

The runway is shown with bright lights marking a 10m wide strip that wheeled spacecraft can use to match the station's rotation. The lights are placed every 5 metres on both edges of the strip, except for one spot where the lights are placed 2m apart (because the runway length is not a multiple of 5m).

An approaching vehicle will be moving at about 7m/s relative to the runway when stationary - weak cold gas thrusters can easily be used to touch down and then brake until the vehicle is stationary on the runway. If a vehicle wants to take off, it only needs to accelerate to 7m/s in the anti-spinwards direction, and then it will be able to leave the station as the centrifugal gravity disappears. The wheels notably do not have to be powered or have brakes, they merely have to function well as wheels, since the force for braking or accelerating can also be provided by the thrusters.

Spacecraft that do not have wheels (which is the standard, nearly no spacecraft need permanently attached wheels) can simply have wheeled palettes attached to them ahead of landing.
