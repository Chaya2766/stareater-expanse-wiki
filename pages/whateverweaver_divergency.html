Whateverweaver divergency

The Whateverweaver divergency is a family of iteratively more advanced mind uploads sourced from one human individual. At the moment the family includes the following:
  1. Patricia, the founder of the family. She had been a transhumanist for a long time before the decision to digitize her mind. After the split, she kept working on some of her projects mainly involving bodily modification.

  2. Felicity, a digital transhuman hyperfocussed on technological independence and full understanding of her own critical infrastructure. She is fundamentally just a digital emulation of Patricia's brain with minor additions, and also exists in a convergency with 16 copies of herself.

  3. Serendipity, a composite mind with 16 brain nodes, incorporating Felicity as one of its elements. Vastly more mentally capable than either Patricia or Felicity but not so advanced as to become completely unrelatable.


A rough pattern apparent from the family's activity is that they start and complete roughly one major project every gigasecond.

Divergency members

Patricia

Patricia Whateverweaver, a transhumanist and body modder with great appreciation for open source projects. She spent most of her early life working on an artificial full body replacement for herself all the while living a minimalistic lifestyle off of universal basic income and other basic welfare policies she could get access to. She ended up releasing the full blueprint and documentation of her body for free as an open source project after it was sufficiently complete, and went on to travel the solar system, putting her new body to use at various jobs.

Much later, she decided on undergoing an extensive process to precisely measure and model her nervous system with machine learning, creating a computer simulation of herself, which came to be known as Felicity, thus creating the Whateverweaver divergency.

After the split, she kept some of her personal project and gave up some others so that Felicity would have something to live for too, and went on doing mostly the same things as she usually did. Her freely released body project over time gained a personally huge following (though still insignificant at the grand scale of the solar system) and allowed her to benefit from some community-made developments, as well as a small constant inflow of donations.

Felicity

In terms of actual content, Felicity is by far the most developed character out of the divergency. Her full form is a PDF document full of both lore and equations, more than 150 pages in length, painstakingly accumulated across years of research and numerous rewrites stemming from me rigorously rooting out technical discrepancies.
(I plan to eventually upload the document into the repository too, once it is sufficiently finished, and place a link to it here.)

Felicity is a complete software emulation of Patricia's brain and nervous system, made through a crude but easy to understand process that left her knowing how she works better than she ever could as a biological being. Her mind is a massively computationally demanding system that in spite of advanced hardware used, requires whole dedicated buildings much like ancient supercomputers to run, and she must resort to remote bodies when she wishes to interact with people outside of virtual spaces.

Due to the exact structure of her mind, it is possible for her to exist as multiple copies simultaineously and synchronize memories later. She normally exists in a convergency with up to 16 copies of herself, witnessing and remembering events from multiple perspectives, and experiencing herself from the first, second and third person perspectives.

Her main occupation is asteroid mining, where she employs industrial processes she mostly put together with her own research, to extract all the materials needed to produce her own hardware, forming circular supply chains that she is fully in control of. Some of the material and hardware is sold occasionally for extra income or just to support nearby settlements. Every single blueprint and industrial procedure she needs to maintain herself independent from supply chains outside of her control is passionately detailed in a massively overgrown hypertext sometimes referred to as the "Felicity tech tree".

Serendipity

Serendipity is a derivant of Felicity who split away from the convergency due to technical incompatibility, caused by her modifications done to her own brain. She is made up of her core mind, largely identical to that of Felicity, and 15 AGI subminds glued onto it as well as each other with direct neural connections. She does not know where her core mind ends and the subminds begin, the brains had already gotten used to their new connections so well that they are effectively just like neural circuits linking different parts of the same brain, they form one whole that is collectively identified as Serendipity. Within the divergency Serendipity is generally referred to as posthuman, as it is agreed her level of self-modification feels distinctly beyond what is usually referred to when the divergents talk about transhumanism, but she is better thought of as an artificial mind merely imbued with Felicity's personality and other mental characteristics rather than a direct continuation.

Characteristic vocabulary:
  • Overload - a sensation completely distinct from pain but similar to it in effect, which is usually caused by the strength of normally non-noxious stimuli exceeding the brain's rated operating parameters. Notably overload is different from pain in that pain can be located on the body, whereas overload doesn't have a location, or sometimes is said to be located in the mind rather than anywhere on the body, effectively being just pain with none of the physical aspects. It is also different from pain in that pain causes a lot of automatic responses, whereas overload causes only the suffering. This experience and the associated word was originally just a very subjective function of Patricia's neurology, but had been turned into a common feature of the whole family's vocabulary, as them being copies who share (or at least include) the same neurology, they all experience it too, finally giving them somebody else who can relate to it in the exact way they mean it.

Philosophy

What constitutes death (click to expand)
To the whateverweavers, there are at least two distinct types of death, which have different implications and are not mutually exclusive: pattern death and continuity death. continuity death is the end of your ability to experience life - it is what happens when your brain stops working and never starts again. Continuity death typically has a clear boundary - you either did die or didin't, with little room inbetween. When continuity death occurs, you lose that particular line of consciousness - the person who experienced it no longer exists even if a very close copy of them might. That continuous line of consciousness and subjective experiences had come to an end.

pattern death is typically non-binary, you aren't clearly either dead or not but rather you die just a little bit. A total pattern death would mean that you had either ceased to exist, or became something utterly unrecognizable, and nothing that is like the you you considered yourself exists anymore. But pattern death still occurs even if you are recovered - if you died and were restored from a backup made 5 minutes ago, you are still missing those 5 minutes, it is a death of sorts, even if minuscule and fractional, and would be a much bigger but still not total death if you lost 5 years instead. When a total pattern death occurs, all of the things that you could have someday said or done, ideas you could have had, things you could have made others feel, but never did, are lost, and will never be known.

Change to them is a form of death. Every time you forget something, every time you learn something new that shifts your personality a bit, you die a little bit - if you go on forever, someday all of the things you are now will be lost and whoever you will become may be a person that behaves nothing like you, remembers nothing you know, believes different things, doesn't talk to the same people. In accordance with this set of beliefs, that is not you, that is someone else. In practice you will likely never forget some things, some little bit of your past self's behaviour will remain in you no matter how far in the future - in that way, a truly total pattern death by divergence is very unlikely, and very difficult, just as it is difficult to not change at all.

Within the divergency, pattern death is generally more of a concern than continuity death. If it is determined that you did experience a significant pattern death, it would not be inappropriate to hold a symbolic funeral for your old self. Where the old self ended and new self began can only really be determined in hindsight, and will most likely be just an arbitrary choice of where to draw the line on something that has no clear boundary.

Patricia as a single organic being is at risk of experiencing a pattern death due to her maleable brain tissue even if she does not ever experience a continuity death. If she does experience a continuity death, it would also be a pattern death unless she is successfully reconstructed from her neurological data afterwards, which is quite likely thanks to its quality and the existing preparations for that eventuality.

Felicity occasionally suffers continuity deaths, and will suffer many more in the future, but a pattern death is unlikely, because her digital mind is not maleable enough for her behaviour to ever change much from how it is now, and her rigorous routine of backups and data parity checks means that anytime she dies, there is almost certainly a copy of her somewhere out there, and she will come back as her recognizable self.

Identity (click to expand)
Among uploads and divergents, identities are not strictly separate. Patricia made her own body as a major project long before Felicity came to existence as a digital reconstruction of Patricia's mind. Now, even though both of them exist at the same time, it is just as appropriate to say that Patricia completed that project as it is to say that Felicity completed it, because their identities truly overlap in that region - that is when both of them were just the same person, and that past belongs to both of them now.
While most people who had never engaged in mind uploading can get away with thinking of identity in completely binary terms, ie. either the same person or different people, with no inbetween, for the whateverweaver family this way of looking at things is just plainly incompatible with the physical reality of their living situation. Patricia and Felicity used to be the same person and have the same memories of their childhood before having split apart into separate entities in the late 14th gigasecond.

Within the family, identity is thought of in fuzzy terms, ie. Patricia and Felicity are a little bit the same person because they used to be the same before they diverged. It is just as valid to say that Felicity created patricia's body as it is to say that Patricia created her own body, because at that time they were not separate yet.

Similarly it is almost just as valid to say that Serendipity built the finespun tech tree as it is to say that Felicity built it, with the slight difference that it actually isn't acceptable to say that of anything Felicity created after Serendipity became a separate entity.

Felicity who exists as 16 instances of herself, routinely synchronizing their memories, would not be able to function if each one of her was seen as a wholly different person, thus all of them collectively are usually referred to as "Felicity", and sometimes when the distinction is necessary, an individual instance such as "Felicity 12" might be specified. Felicity's multi-instance existence also makes it very difficult to tell apart having an instance die before she could synchronize with others from having simply forgotten that something happened, thus for her death and forgetting are simply different points on the same spectrum - if she dies 5 minutes after having synchronized her instances, she would not see it differently than having simply forgotten about 5 minutes of her existence, and if she lost an entire month of memories she would think of it similarly to the version of her who remembered that time having died even if strictly speaking she just forgot all of it instead of having physically died.

This model of death is also widely adopted throughout the family since it builds on ideas that had already existed in Patricia even before her first mind uploading. It also places Patricia in a unique situation among the family members - her brain, unlike the digital models that run Felicity and Serendipity, is still fully fluid. Like in any human brain, Patricia's memories change just a little bit every time she recalls them, her personality drifts just a tiny bit every day, some things are more resistant to change than others but eventually all of her memories, and all of her characteristic behaviour, will have changed, and at that point by the family's definitions, patricia will be dead despite her brain having never ceased to work, and whoever she will be then may be thought of as a new member of the family.

Felicity, due to the way she works, is a sort of immortal snapshot of late 14th gigasecond Patricia, because her digital brain emulation is built with a largely static structure, and allows some memory formation but most parts of her brain will never change, she will never unlearn certain habits or develop permanent new personality quirks, and this is seen as ideal, whereas Patricia's natural personality drift is thought of like a slow degenerative disease even though it is a natural function of the human brain, and thus Patricia continues to get updated brain scans and collect neural activity for an eventual another future upload or whatever new alternatives become available to "fix" it.
