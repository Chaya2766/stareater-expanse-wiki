Unix epoch

Unix epoch is a date and time system that includes no days, weeks, months or years and tracks time as simply the number of seconds passed since 1970-01-01 00:00:00 UTC. Many habitats had adopted it as the time system of choice for coordination, especially where no day-night cycle is implemented, where habitats use different cycle lengths, and it remains the main competitor to the gregorian calendar.

Kiloseconds, megaseconds and gigaseconds are generally used as common units of time among those who adhere to this timekeeping system, though typically shortened for ease of casual use such as saying "kays" or less commonly "kilos" instead of "kiloseconds". For the purpose of making dates more human readable it is most common to space out the digits in groups of 3 to implicitly specify which are the seconds and which the kilo- mega- and giga seconds.

Dates before time zero are simply written as negative, for example the first human footstep on the moon was made at unix time -14 115 840, corresponding to 02:56 UTC on July 21, 1969.
