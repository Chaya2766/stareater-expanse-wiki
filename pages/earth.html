Terra

Terra is the emblematic example of a human-habitable planet. In other contexts it is also known as Earth.

In the modern day the vast majority of people who would consider themselves Terra's population actually live in the orbital habitat cloud around the planet, as the planet itself is just too thermally and spatially constrained to house all the people wishing to live there. Terra's population hovers around 500 billion people.

In the context of the solar system, it is often seen as a luxury destination, a shrine to humanity's beginnings, or the home of those powerful enough to earn their place on it.

Inhabitation-relevant characteristics

  • Total population: 500 billion ± 4%
  • Average population density: 980/km2
  • Urbanized area ≈ 29% (≈ 50% of land and ≈ 20% of ocean)

Terran infrastructure

Vast majority of terran infrastructure is living space, with industry and bulk computation mostly moved into orbit, resulting in a very low average of 20kW power use per inhabitant if only surface infrastructure is counted, and placing it very precisely at kardashev 1 status. Counting orbital infrastructure however, the number grows substantially, as the total terran planetary swarm is said to capture and use about 0.04% of the light that comes closer than the orbit of Luna.

The transit between surface and space is handled using tethered rings, somewhat counterintuitive constructs which cleverly utilize the curvature of the earth to hang high up above the ground by hanging from the ground. I'll limit my explanation here to saying that the ring is kept rigid with active support, and physically pulled along the surface to a higher latitude where the diameter of the ring is larger than the diameter of earth directly below it. For a more elaborate explanation see Isaac Arthur's video on the topic here and/or the Atlantis Project website. Electrically powered surface to orbit transport nearly negates its own cost, as cargo coming down to the surface actually generates power instead of consuming it, power which can be used directly to raise cargo moving in the other direction.
