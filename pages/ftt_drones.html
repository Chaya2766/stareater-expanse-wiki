Felicity Techtree: Drones

Drones are remotely controlled bodies. Software provided with the Felicity tech tree abstracts away the details of controlling a remote body into a simple universal interface that is well fit for both AI and uploaded minds.

Control software

In the Felicity tech tree, the software package that enables using drone bodies simply runs a webserver that accepts and returns image files. Bodily control such as motor positions, muscle tension, activation of various tools and other such things, are all just mapped onto various areas of an image that should be sent to the droid body. Sensors of the body, such as view from cameras or pressure data from sensors on the skin, and any other desired returns, can be mapped onto an image that the webserver makes available for download.

In principle even an old web browser could view this interface, and a determined enough person could control a drone body this way, but that would be very awkward, as such the software on the other end is typically used to convert output neuron activations of an AI model or the nerve signals of an emulated brain into an image and send it to the drone body automatically, usually at 100 to 1000 times per second (which depending on the configured resolution can require bandwidth in the hundreds of gigabytes per second). The usual security measure is simply a password that is configured for accessing the drone body, much in the same manner as one would set the password on a wi-fi router.

Light drone

Fairly common drone body to see operated by UEIA or sometimes by Felicity herself. It is almost entirely made up of 3d-printed parts, including the skeleton, motors, electronics and batteries (made with metal innert gas 3d printing and/or Ion beam printing). Its rated carrying capacity assumes that 4 limbs are used as legs and the other 4 as arms, but if need arises, all 8 could be used as legs to double the carrying capacity. This is helpful in case the drone needs to operate in any environment with acceleration greater than 8.54m/s2, where the drone's own weight surpasses its normal carrying capacity.

Despite being somewhat impractical, the design remains in use due to emotional attachment. Here is a document detailing how the values listed in the table on the left were decided.

Humanoid-shaped drone body primarily for use by Felicity herself.

4 arms + 2 legs included for locomotion and manipulation purposes, plus a twinned tail which can be used to anchor to appropriately shaped parts of the environment. 4 large wings are included, which aid movement in weightless and low-gravity environments. The skin is covered in short fur, which is also removable for washing.

The sensory capabilities of the drone body are in mismatch with that of a normal human body and they are not meant to be perceived without an intermediate sensory translation layer. 6 eyes located all around the head enable viewing in all directions at once, each equipped with several monochromatic cameras tuned to different wavelengths as well as a cosmetic colour-adjustable glowing iris. 6 independently targetable ears allow sound sources in the environment to be located and isolated from other noise.

The use of numerous monochromatic cameras instead of single trichromatic cameras causes notable chromatic aberration when looking at close objects, but also allows them to be configured into nonstandard trichromacy such as 720nm(IR)+575nm(yellow)+320nm(UV) or other unusual combinations.
