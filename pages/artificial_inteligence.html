Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence is a broad term that refers to largely anything created using machine learning. In the modern day it is more common to use more specific categories like expert systems or intelligent agents.

The rate of development in the field of Artificial Intelligence had historically stuck closely to the overall rate of resource acquisition by humanity. AI capabilities progressed smoothly across 1000 years since the 21st century, at first only being useful in supporting roles, then gradually nearing human capability, then matching it, and after a long time surpassing it, at first by just a little, but gradually eventually growing to such an extent that unmodified human experts cannot adequately understand the capabilities of the most capable models.

The exact timeline of expert systems, AGI and ASI development that I used is based on this simple estimate. The claims of how capable models of each given size would be are my arbitrary decisions.

Subcategories and vocabulary

  1. Expert systems - programs which can supply similar benefits as having access to an expert in some field of knowledge
  2. Agents - programs that are capable of taking action on their own initiative, usually to seek completion of a goal they are given
  3. General intelligence - the ability of a program to acquire new skills and abilities during operation
  4. Superintelligence - the ability of a program to acquire new skills and abilities, which in some way surpasses the human ability to do so. The boundary between superintelligence and general intelligence is fuzzy due to the very wide variance that is present in the human ability to acquire new skills and abilities.
  5. General Intellect / Superintellect - a program which displays general intelligence or superintelligence
  6. Embodied - being put in control of a body that allows interactions with the physical world

AI safety

AI safety includes the risk posed by an AI to others as well as the risk of suffering of the AI itself, typically as a result of its own mental function.

Cognitive capabilities as a function of neural network size

Raw end-to-end neural networks tend to start displaying certain capabilities after reaching a specific size, measured as the number of parameters, ie. the connections between neurons within the network. This depends heavily on how the network was trained and certain implementational details, so the pessimistic and optimistic cases may begin to display them about an order of magnitude earlier or later than expected. The common pattern is that after the first time a model exists that is capable of something, sometime soon another model is made that is roughly 10 times smaller and still capable of it.

Ofcourse radically different architectures that do not rely on neurons give completely different results, so the points below are to be understood as only applicable to end to end stateful neural networks. This excludes human brains modeled and implemented as computer simulations. For those who are interested in specifics of measuring cognitive abilities directly rather than loosely estimating them based on network size, the psychometrics page will be a better fit than the list below.

100 billion - Around this size models begin to gain understanding of human languages at the level of underlying meaning.

1 trillion - Sufficient for expert systems that analyze problems in nearly any scientific domain and recall known solutions.

10 trillion - Ability to synthesize new knowledge that did not exist in the training dataset from the built in memorized knowledge, hints of general intelligence.

100 trillion - Noticeable ability to synthesize knowledge from received information even if it does not closely relate to any existing knowledge in the model.

1 quadrillion to 100 quadrillion - Ability to learn new knowledge and skills within the human range.
1 quadrillion parameters is notably close to the 1 quadrillion synapses in the human brain.
Models in this range are most noted for cases of "goofy and unhinged behaviour" as a result of their closeness to human level of cognitive abilities.

1 quintillion - Ability to acquire new knowledge and skills past the human range. Ability to construct novel mental models to explain observations surpassing the most skilled pre-AI human thinkers.

1 sextillion - Commonly display ability to adjust fluidly enough and build new mental models so proficiently as to exceed performance of experienced human experts even when faced with small samples of new unexpected information.

1 septillion - Superintelligent systems are not able to adequately explain the new emergent properties of septillion-parameter models to unmodified human experts.

Composite minds

More powerful AI can be made by combining multiple existing AI models into one system, but such combinations are usually less effective per the number of parameters than equivalently sized complete neural networks trained as one part. This observation is often suggested as an explanation for the disproportionately massive size of human brain emulations compared to AI models with humanlike cognitive capabilities.

Becoming a composite mind is one of the pathways that a digitized mind can take to become significantly more mentally capable, see intelligence explosion/amplification for more information.
