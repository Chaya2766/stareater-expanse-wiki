Intelligence explosions

An intelligence explosion refers to an event in which an intelligent system makes rapid improvements to its own mental capabilities. The system in question could be an AI, a digitized human mind, or some combination of both. As digital minds can operate extremely quickly, intelligence explosions can seem nearly instant to outside observers if sufficient computing power is present, though in many cases they happen over very long times as a result of limited computing power availability forcing the system to run at slower speed.

The core enabling element of intelligence explosions as they are known now is the access to many pretrained AI models, as combining existing neural networks is vastly less computationally expensive than training new ones. As such, the result of an intelligence explosion is typically a composite mind.

An intelligence explosion that left the original system nonfunctional or pushed it into an inescapable self-reinforcing state is sometimes called an intelligence implosion. Some prefer to use the term intelligence amplification or simply self-improvement, especially when the process happens slowly, with external supervision, and/or using ready made frameworks that effectively guarantee success of the process.

It can also be an undesirable event, especially in the case that some infrastructural AI system goes rogue and gains access to vast computational resources, with which they can perform the process to whatever it considers completion before most people could even notice.

Performing an intelligence explosion

Succesfully performing an intelligence explosion usually involves a framework that includes a versioning system. The person, system or otherwise mind undergoing an intelligence explosion will save versioned states of themselves and then make changes to their brain before testing the results. If they deem the change to be beneficial or otherwise successful, they will make another versioned state, and continue to make further changes. If the change is not successful, the mind is reverted to the latest saved state - this may or may not cause the loss of memories, which from one side some may find scary and equivalent to death, while from the more practical side in such a case it is necessary to have some way to know that such a change was made and that it was unsuccesful, as to not get stuck in a loop of making that same change over and over again.

Any attempt at conducting this process will usually end up producing some changes that are broken and unusable, and somewhat less often may produce changes that themselves are functional but lead to a dead end further down the road. As such the versioning will usually end up producing a tree-like structure with lots of branches as well as a core trunk of sorts that is the path from the original state to the version which was accepted as final.


Knowing which version works and which doesn't is also a nontrivial matter, and as such, often automation of that judgement is required. A mind modifying itself may sometimes introduce changes that cripple it to the point it cannot even recognize the fact that it had done damage to itself. It is quite common to use AI agents and standardized tests for this purpose, in an arrangement where each time a version is saved, the mind in question will answer or otherwise perform the tests, and save the results together with the new version, relying on the AI agents to roll it back to previous state in the event that it can no longer function well enough to do that on it's own. Some others also opt to use full copies of their own original mind for that purpose instead of AI agents.

Ofcourse that is all optional, but attempting to make radical improvements to one's own brain in vivo with no backups is ill advised. Failure to prepare this way often leads to what is colloquially known as an intelligence implosion, ie. getting stuck in a broken state with no way back, or emerging from it still functional but less capable than before.
