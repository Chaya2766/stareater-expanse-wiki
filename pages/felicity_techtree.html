Felicity techtree

A collection of blueprints and procedures that together form a self-sufficient whole, capable of producing working equipment from any of the provided blueprints given only solar energy and asteroid material.

This is the result of Felicity's combined curiosity and aversion to relying on technology she does not understand, that pushed her to put a lifetime of work into developing a complete technological system that is simultaneously sufficient for its own needs and simple enough for a homo terran mind to understand.

The conceptual simplicity often comes at a great cost in efficiency in terms of energy or time as well as scalability. Most of the industrial processes are executed on a small scale, using machines that a single person can operate.

Low Temperature Electrolysis of mixed metal oxides

Low Temperature Electrolysis refining can be thought of as a precursor to a whole big branch of the tech tree Felicity operates, as it trivializes the extraction of several metals from asteroid material.

Asteroid dust is mixed in with a concentrated solution of sodium hydroxide at close to 100oC and a current is passed through it, reducing the metal oxides from the dust into a mixed alloy of these metals. The resulting alloy is roughly equivalent to wrought iron, which can be later refined into the elemental metals or simply used as is, but it is not corrosion-resistant.

Once the process is done, the cathode will have grown a beautiful and heavy tree of crystallized metal that can be lifted out of the machine and scraped off for further processing. A large amount of oxygen is also produced in the process, and a lot of elements cannot be reduced to their elemental form like this, so they will be left in a sludge of dust at the bottom that needs to be cleaned out and maybe refined in some other way.

Among those elements which are left unextracted are some very important ones like chromium, which is needed for stainless steel, or lithium, which is needed for high-performance batteries. These lacking elements are the reason that the LTE process can be thought of as a precursor to it's own branch in the tech tree, as the specific set of elements it provides forces a lot of downstream design choices that would have otherwise made no sense.

Ion beam deposition printing

This is one of many things that Felicity did not bother re-inventing herself, as the complexity of the machinery required simply overpowered the need for deep understanding when sufficiently open source solutions already existed.

Using ion beams to deposit material in exact quantities at exact positions can create macroscopic objects given enough time. The exact printer model that Felicity included in the tech tree can achieve deposition rates of 5 micromol per second, which means that print times typically vary from hours to weeks with some exceptions. The willingness to make such an extreme payment of time and energy (since the machine is pulling about 1020 watts the whole time if printing at max speed) for the ability to produce complex electronics and photovoltaics from a single machine is fairly typical of Felicity's approach to technological independence.

Calutron separation

Calutron separation is the universal method for acquiring isotopically pure elements.

The calutron refinery included in the tech tree uses electron bombardment to ionize the input material, then accelerates the ions and passes them through a magnetic field, which causes the ion stream to deflect, with the exact angle of deflection depending on the mass of the ion - as such the material is sorted exactly by atomic mass, ending up in one of 267 collection tubes. Elements purified in such a way are fit for use as feedstock for the ion beam printers.

It generally costs from tens to hundreds of gigajoules to decompose a kilogram of matter into its constituent elements in this manner, with the exact number depending on the exact composition of the material being taken apart.

Kamacite baking

Calutron extraction of trace elements from kamacite is extremely inefficient, as such an intermediate process had been introduced which greatly concentrates the valuable trace elements using their vapor pressure. Kamacite baking leaves kamacite wire heated in an oven chamber so that the trace elements submlimate out of it and deposit on the cooler chamber walls, after numerous repetitions the built up crust of valuable elements is scraped off and put through calutron separation. This is an absolutely critical process to achieve reasonable rates of pure element production.

Gallium arsenide photovoltaics

Gallium Arsenide photovoltaics are the primary power source for Felicity. These can be made simply with the ion beam deposition process. At great distances where sunlight is faint, they would be used as a small part of larger concentrated solar arrays that can boost the power production by 23.5 times while costing only about 1.5 times as much energy (but much more material) to produce than plain solar panels. Ofcourse a concentrated solar array has the downside of having to stay pointed accurately at the sun, which is largely negated by operating in weightlessness.

microwave phased arrays

Microwave Phased Arrays serve communication as well as wireless transfer of power, operating at 5.8GHz with 86.6% efficiency. These can be made simply with the ion beam deposition process.

Made in tiles just like the gallium arsenide solar cells, microwave phased arrays solve the problem of getting power from solar arrays to rotating habitats without a physical connection. About 25% of the power transmitted is lost in end to end transmission, so it is still preferable to put equipment directly on the concentrated solar arrays instead when possible.

Hydrogen peroxide synthesis

High-concentration hydrogen peroxide (also called HTP for "high-test peroxide") can be used as a monopropellant and works adequately well as fuel for travel between destinations within a planet's sphere of influence. The supplied machine for HTP production uses a process that involves passing current through water while repeatedly flipping its polarity to produce 10% hydrogen peroxide, and then uses vacuum distillation to concentrate it to 85%.

HTP can be handled well with monel, an alloy of nickel and copper, which notably can both be acquired from the LTE process. Simple thrusters can be fairly easily 3d-printed from monel, using either catalytic or thermal decomposition to extract the energy from the hydrogen peroxide. Piping and fuel tanks can also be made from monel, and must use welded seams.

Avoiding HTP leakage is of extreme importance given that the primary construction material in this tech tree is a wrought-iron-like alloy vulnerable to corrosion. Any amount of HTP spillage usually results in blood red rust covering the affected wall or floor in less than a minute.

Compute infrastructure

The compute infrastructure provided by the tech tree consists of 3 main parts:
  • Compute nodes equipped with large amounts of general purpose computing chips and standard compute-sharing software capable of handling homomorphic encryption. Client nodes can connect to them and delegate bulk computation to be performed by these nodes, all the while homomorphic encryption allows the computation to be performed without actually revealing the real data to the compute nodes.
  • Network nodes provide the communication primarily via infrared laser pulses, but also through some other means. The network is self-organizing and nodes automatically detect each other upon line of sight to make the setup easier.
  • Program nodes run the actual AI programs and other critical software in plain form (no homomorphic encryption). They themselves don't have anywhere near enough computing power for most applications, and as such must rely on the compute nodes to do bulk of the processing. The main purpose of the program nodes is security - they are hardened against tampering, external intrusion, and immediately erase their contents when physically compromised.
These elements form a scalable compute grid that makes any settlement using it practically its own local distributed supercomputer.

Power infrastructure

The power infrastructure in the tech tree runs on 576 volts DC and provides a universal connector that carries both power and coolant. Solar power, battery storage, water cooling and cheap kamacite cables make up the basis of the power grid. Management of waste heat (ie. thermal power) is considered a part of the power infrastructure.

Organic Chemistry subtree Work In Progress

This portion of the tech tree is tasked primarily with production of the one critically important flexible insulator, polyethylene. It deals with chemicals that are far too complex to be produced by clever mixing of individual elements during ion beam printing.

Ethanol synthesis

Ethanol is synthesized from raw material without incorporating living beings like yeast in the process. Doing this involves burning carbon with oxygen to produce carbon dioxide, mixing it with hydrogen at an exact ratio, compressing it to a precise required pressure and passing it over an ion-beam-printed iron-carbon catalyst while heated to a precise needed temperature to cause the reactions that produce ethanol.

At the end, the ethanol is distilled and concentrated, so that the main end result of this process is liquid ethanol with very little water content.

Ethylene production

This is a relatively simple process which involves vaporizing ethanol, compressing it to a reasonable pressure and passing it across a precisely heated aluminium oxide as a catalyst before compressing it. As a bonus to being a step in the production of polyethylene, ethylene is a pretty good refrigerant.

Polyethylene production

Polymerization of Polyethylene is done with a silica-supported chromium oxide catalyst (also known as a "philips catalyst"), produced with the help of ion-beam printing and thermal treatment.
