Basics of the setting

This page should be interpreted as outside commentary on the setting
Stareater Expanse places extreme pressure on scientific accuracy and logic-driven development of the setting above "cool" writing, with ofcourse the notable approach that many of the fictional characters want cool things and as such sometimes make cool things even if they don't make sense - this is a pattern that shows heavily in the writing and is extensively (though still within reason) used to justify "cool" things that would not make sense from the strictly practical perspective.

The path of humanity is set by a simple extrapolation, assuming that the apparent exponential growth following the industrial revolution will continue for at least a thousand years, forcing rapid expansion, diversification and creative engineering as means to accomodate the growing humanity and emerging posthumanity.

Despite the name of the setting, it is located almost entirely within the solar system, though some workarounds are available for accessing astronomically distant interesting locations such as the Spider Globular Cluster shown on the right.

Precursor writing projects

  • Stars From was a world set in the Omega Centauri globular cluster, using the density of stars as a justification for fast interstellar travel as well as presence of multiple naturally evolved sapient aliens within a volume of just a few hundred light years. The primary focus on the setting was the investigation of nonhuman minds, which honed my skills in writing human-comprehensible descriptions of emotions that humans do not experience and avoiding arbitrarily human behaviours in nonhuman characters, culminating in aliens such as the citwaz. Correspondingly, the cluster was to be inhabited by an entirely nonhuman civilization, and largely made no attempts at soothing the discomfort caused by being surrounded with only beings that do not display many crucial human behaviours or share human goals, but unfortunately this led to very little interest, and over time made even me grow a desire to finally write something a human can not just understand but also relate to.
    Such deep exploration of the inhuman had also shown me that the human is not generic, but unique, and in many ways very interesting too, resulting in my next project:

  • Sol25 presented my vision of the human society and the solar system in the 25th century, largely centered by my character Patricia, who was a major upgrade in the extent to which I actually rely on calculated details in my writing. Ironically it felt very much like constraining myself to only the solar system conveyed an overwhelming sense of scale that an entire globular cluster could not.
Stareater Expanse reuses content from these projects as well as the skills I acquired working on them, and the exploration of naturally occurring inhuman intelligent minds is still planned. But in current efforts, the most important thing I try to convey is a sense of scale - Stareater Expanse is meant to be the exact oposite of the common sci-fi writing pattern where each planet in a sci-fi universe is depicted by a single town, assigned a common culture, visited for just for a moment. The amount of space available in the solar system or even any single planet is truly overwhelming, and I want to show it off.

Beyond Sol

As of the time this is being written (27-01-2026) no content had been explicitly located in places light years removed from the solar system, but a plot device had been established to enable stories which would lead to such locations.
