Advanced Recovery Code Standard

With the massive amounts of data routinely used and stored in the modern day, corruption is routine and expected. Although widespread and well-supported, it is just one of many schemes used to protect data against corruption.

ARCS is a standard specifying a scheme for packaging any piece of data with a recovery code capable that helps in error detection and allows the original bits to be recreated in case of corruption. Digital files sometimes incorporate a built-in ARCS field, with the help of which they undergo routine integrity checks and repair. Other times, for files which do not support having a built-in ARCS field, separate arcs files can be generated to provide them with redundancy.

It incorporates a number of algorithms, most notably Reed-Solomon error correction codes. In the future will likely add an option to pick between (or maybe use both) reed-solomon and Low-Density Parity Checks (I expect that LDPC would be much better for massive files thanks to linear complexity - Reed-Solomon appears to be completely unuseable for error correction on files zetabytes in size such as human brain emulations even for a quettabithertz level compute cluster, and even for particularly large AI models stays prohibitively expensive - but I should fix the bitflip cost calculation of Reed-Solomon first)

Commonly used values

  • 0.5% redundancy relative to original: results in files that are 1% bigger than their unsecured counterparts
  • 5% redundancy relative to original: results in files that are 10% bigger than their unsecured counterparts
  • 10% redundancy across the whole file: results in files that are 25% bigger than their unsecured counterparts
  • 25% redundancy across the whole file: results in files that are twice as big as their unsecured counterparts
