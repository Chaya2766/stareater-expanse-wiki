Felicity Techtree: Kamacite baking

Calutron extraction of trace elements from kamacite is extremely inefficient, as such an intermediate process had been introduced which greatly concentrates the valuable trace elements using their vapor pressure.

document with math behind the process can be found here

Extraction

A small oven chamber of about 1 cubic metre in volume is built, with positive and negative terminals on the inside for supplying controlled amounts of power. The chamber has a hanging rack on the inside, within which there is a temperature sensor. 1612 metres of millimetre-thick kamacite wire is measured out and hung up on the rack, with the ends connected to the terminals so that current can be pushed through the wire. This length of 1mm wire adds up to slightly more than 10kg of kamacite, and has just a little over 5m2 of total surface area.

The chamber is closed and set to 1500 kelvin temperature, then left for about 10 kiloseconds. During that time it will be consuming a constant 87kW of power to maintain its temperature, which ends up consuming 870MJ in total. It will then have to be left to cool for another 10 kiloseconds.

While the chamber is hot, elements with low vapor pressures will sublimate out of the kamacite wire and deposit themselves on the cooler chamber walls. This process should be repeated about 74 times with fresh new batches of wire, and then the material deposited on the inside of the chamber is thoroughly scaped off into a separate container - there should be about 1kg of total material on the walls of the chamber at that point, and the amount of energy used to produce this much kamacite bake would have by now been 64.38GJ.
