Behaviour

The noomote becomes hyperfocussed on a single task upon being given one and can't reliably multitask. E will rely heavily on information within eir field of view rather than working from memory, as e cannot remember large amounts of information. Most of eir actions stem from some form of instinct, which can accomplish many things that require intelligence in humans, but e does also have a small capacity for broad complex reasoning which e will occasionally apply. Over time a noomote which had done the same task repeatedly will specialize and remember details about the task that allow em to perform better at that task in the future.

Noomotes can team up and often show spontaneous cooperation when encountering each other unexpectedly. Spontaneous organization often produces structures with workers, managers, inspectors and philosophers, whose jobs can be roughly described:
  • workers - perform unit tasks with absolute dedication, problem-solving to completion of their narrowly defined goal. In case of having a particularly severe realization while working on their task, they may diversify into managers, inspectors or philosophers.
  • managers - divide up projects into small tasks and assign them to workers or lower managers, keeping track of the progress and reporting to higher managers as needed
  • inspectors - inspect the tasks being assigned and methods that the workers are using to complete those tasks to detect any action which goes outside the bounds of the project or violates some rule, as well as thoroughly documenting how the project is ran. Occasionally remind workers and managers that had forgotten their task what they were meant to be doing.
  • philosophers - theorize about implicit constraints and explore how the project could fail to fulfill its intent despite achieving its goal as stated. Their job is to inform the inspectors what they should be looking out for to prevent cases where the methods used to achieve given goals are causing more harm than the task or project was worth in the first place.
These roles are only vaguely applied and the boundaries are fuzzy, it is also expected that small amounts of noomotes will take on roles which fit into none of these categories.

Noomotes typically need to communicate with a more cognitively capable mind such as UEIA to stay on track of a single project. When not managed, their actions tend to diversify and broaden, typically resulting in them performing various maintenance tasks and documenting their virtual surroundings.

Even though noomotes are highly adapted for working in swarms, even a single noomote is actually quite capable of completing small projects, especially when dealing with pure information work such as making software or collecting and processing information.
