Finespun

Finespun is a small mining settlement in the solar system asteroid belt, inhabited in majority by UEIA workers and a single human brain emulation known as Felicity.

A unique feature of this settlement is its reliance on a custom ecosystem of technology that all together allows any piece of itself to be produced from just sunlight and asteroid regolith all the while remaining simple enough that every single worker on Finespun understands every single piece of equipment.

Despite its absurd tech ecosystem, the settlement is capable of comfortably maintaining a growth rate of ×20 per gigasecond, and can achieve growth rates as high as ×27 per gigasecond if all the resources are poured into further expanding its power and production infrastructure, while absolutely isolated from the broader economy.

The settlement's growth can be supercharged by direct financial support, which at the extreme allows it to just barely surpass a growth rate of 100% annually, about ×3921498556 per gigasecond - in that arrangement the entirety of the funding is spent on shipments of high purity gallium and arsenic for solar cells, while all other materials are still produced locally as usual.

The 20× and 27× growth rates were determined experimentally on 26-03-2026 by playing the finespun economic simulator (which is currently still work in progress) for 1 gigasecond of ingame time. The annual doubling was found around unix time 1 780 237 925 after the trade mechanic was partially implemented, but the cost of such growth couldn't be determined because I used bulk prices for elements instead of prices for high purity elements.
After implementing autoplay in the finespun economic simulator, which is capable of performing the same actions I would but without getting bored and clicking wait multiple times in a row to make things go faster or making occasional mistakes, the upper bound on finespun's growth rate without financial assistance increased to somewhere a bit less than 15% annually and more than 70× gigasecondly. Maybe this provides some valid empirical data on how much growth rate loss is expected as a result from mismanagement by the human in charge?

Name and flag

The settlement was named Finespun after the loose composition of the asteroid that centers it, and the architecture that would be built within it in the form of a vast web of tunnels, which ended up never actually being built.

The flag of finespun is an abstract depiction of a kamacite crystal, showing off the mineral's 3-way symmetry, chosen for the symbol due to kamacite being the primary building material for nearly all of Finespun infrastructure, by far the most produced material locally, and also a key element of the local technology.

Finespun officially opened right at the beginning of the 15-gigasecond epoch, with financial backing from ITON, which was spent pretty much entirely on shipments of high purity gallium and arsenic for construction of the settlement's concentrated solar arrays. The funding continued until 32 000 solar arrays were completed, producing a total 8 gigawatts of power, with the gallium and arsenic totaling roughly 5.9 billion dollars equivalent. Further growth was fueled by the settlement's own production, in pretty much complete isolation from the embercold region's economy apart from very occasional exchanges.

The end of funding is visibly reflected in the growth rate as from epoch time 15 000 000 000 to 15 469 607 000 the settlement maintained a 100% annual growth rate (×3921498556 growth per gigasecond), but after the funding stopped, the remaining growth progressed at only 9.9% annual growth rate (×20 growth per gigasecond). After reaching kardashev-0.5 power production, the settlement stopped growing as it was considered to have reached final size at that point, and began to produce excess material.

Vast majority of the excess material would end up sold off to Noca Bell which would benefit greatly from the multiple tonnes of isotopically pure elements that the settlement produces every megasecond. If sold at market prices, the settlement's production would bring in about 641.6 million dollar equivalent every megasecond, 1.84 billion $e annually or about 58.239 $e every second on average.

timekeeping

The local standard of timekeeping is fundamentally tied to Unix Time, and dates are written as just the current epoch time with digits spaced in groups of three. For most purposes, to specify when something will happen, when a task should be done, or leaving a leaving a mark of when something was done, the unix timestamp is the only date format that should be used. The habitat does actually make some use of its own custom calendar regardless of that fact, which spans an entire gigasecond.

short cycle = 100 kiloseconds (= 27 hours, 46 minutes and 40 seconds).
This is what is locally considered a single day. (one of the small boxes on the right)
medium cycle = 1 megasecond (≈ 11.574 terran days) = exactly 10 local days.
This is what is locally considered a week. (the longer horizontal boxes with dotted border)
long cycle = 10 megaseconds (≈ 3.8 gregorian months) = 10 local weeks = 100 local days.
This is what is locally considered a month, and is considered a good amount of time for completing one minor project.
gigasecond (≈ 31.688 terran years) = 100 long cycles = 1000 local weeks = 10 000 local days.
This is treated as the equivalent of a year and considered a good amount of time for completing a major project.

A notable oddity of the finespun calendar is starting the count of days at 0 instead of 1, which means that there is a 0th day of the week, 0th week of the month and a 0th month of the year. The reason for this is to make the unix timestamp trivially readable as a calendar date - coloured parts of the timestamp 16 785 083 980 correspond to the gigasecond, number of the long cycle in the gigasecond, number of the week in the long cycle, day of the week and time of day or alternatively just day of the year.
That example timestamp would correspond to time 83 980th second of the 0th day of the 5th week of the 78th long cycle of the 16th gigasecond.

The 0th day of each week is considered a backup day - it is the day everyone gets for their own use, mainly for things like making backups of important data and/or their minds, running parity checks, equalizing charge levels in their remote body's batteries, etc. Felicity's idea of the backup day is a way to ensure that no more than a week of work can be lost at a time, thus it is done first at the beginning of each week, saving all new experiences, updated hardware designs, any changes to protocols that run the settlement, and the minds of the people living within, so that if anything catastrophic should happen later in the week, anyone participating will have at most lost that week of work and/or life.

Visitors are not expected to follow this convention, and it is generally suggested to follow a 90-kilosecond day cycle (equal exactly to 25 hours) which tends to be more comfortable for humans and other animals aligned with the terran day-night cycle length (the abundance of artificial lighting at finespun habitats tend to slightly delay the onset of sleep in humans and other animals, thus 25 hours actually ends up feeling more natural to follow than the standard 24 hours). Since all timekeeping is based on the unix epoch timestamp, any custom cycle length can be tracked by simply applying it as a modulo to the timestamp to get current time of day ( timestamp % cycle_length = current_time ) which many digital clocks can be easily configured to follow.

160278 Finespun is an S-type asteroid belonging to the Tirela family of asteroids. It has enough mass to produce its own hill sphere, allowing it to have its own orbital infrastructure up to about 30km away from its surface.

The matter of fact standard for where to place orbiting objects around the asteroids is an altitude of 10km from the asteroid's center of mass, where one orbit takes about 16 552 seconds and the orbital velocity is around 3.796m/s.

The asteroid does not have enough surface gravity to walk on, and surface movement has to be limited to less than 5m/s or else risk entering an orbit. This fact is frequently exploited by the mining workers who simply manually throw pods full of regolith onto the a trajectory intersecting the orbital station that will take it in for refining. Cold gas thrusters using waste oxygen from regolith refining tend to be largely sufficient for most purposes given the small orbital velocities.

Nearby objects of interest

160278 Finespun experiences close encounters with asteroid number 36735 every 290 terran years ≈ 9.15Gs. The close encounters within this millennium happen specifically in the gregorian years 2169, 2459, 2749 and one in 3039 earliest next millennium. At the closest their distance from each other is about 0.04 AU ≈ 19.96 light seconds ≈ 5.984E+9 metres.

Culture

Environmental design

Safety-relevant equipment is often placed even where it is not needed just in case that it might be needed someday. Useful infrastructure is sometimes built in seemingly random places just for the sake of there being useful infrastructure. By administrator Felicity's guidance, wherever the people of Finespun stay, there should be useful infrastructure left behind, especially infrastructure that could be useful in an emergency, for the sake of anyone who may end up in the same places in the future. This rule is applied even beyond the Finespun territory, such as when conducting mining expeditions to other asteroids, as such many of the nearby space objects are littered with obvious signs that someone had been there. Occasionally the more extensive examples of such abandoned infrastructure may include AI that decided to remain active within it to perform minimal maintenance, observe the society from a distance and await anyone who may visit in need of help.

Local standards

The adopted audible environmental alert consists of a sinewave tone held constant at 500Hz for 0.25 seconds, then raised in frequency by one whole octave (up to 1000Hz) across 0.5 seconds, and then held constant at 1000Hz for 0.75 seconds, followed by 3 seconds of silence, repeated as long as the environmental emergency is present. Task assignment uses a particular format shown below. This is used for both workers and visitors, a core of this system being the idea that as thinking beings, the recipients of such task specification cards will be able to inference implicit details based on being told why something is being done and the context around it, which would be very hard to explain explicitly.
Task Reason
  • precise description of the task
  • where to go
  • what to do
  • when to do it
  • how to do it

    • Specifying tasks is the most basic element of every plan
  • bigger picture
  • why your task is important
  • what will happen to others if you succeed
  • what will happen to others if you fail

    • Explaining why something is being done has a tendency to convey a lot of important information about how it should be done and what to do in case of unexpected events or circumstances
Coordination Context
  • who to report to with success, failure, progress or problems
  • how to maintain contact
  • what to do if you cannot make contact

    • Communication about tasks has an excessively large effect on the overall quality of work and ensuring it can happen is critical enough and hard enough to deserve its own field. Others must know what you're going to do even when they cannot see or contact you
  • the basics and the obvious
  • who you are working for
  • who you are and why you've been selected
  • free card for any other information you want to provide
  • even if something sounds too obvious, just write enough here to fill the field

    • Intelligent agents tend to organize randomly, and often end up with strange gaps in their knowledge and especially can miss information that is so obvious that nobody bothers to mention it explicitly, so providing even random semi-relevant scraps of information will stop these gaps from accumulating to the point of failure
