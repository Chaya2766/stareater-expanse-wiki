Finespun

Finespun is a small mining settlement in the solar system asteroid belt, inhabited in majority by UEIA workers and a single human brain emulation known as Felicity.

A unique feature of this settlement is its reliance on a custom ecosystem of technology that all together allows any piece of itself to be produced from just sunlight and asteroid regolith all the while remaining simple enough that every single worker on Finespun understands every single piece of equipment.

Despite its absurd tech ecosystem, the settlement had shown itself able to comfortably maintain a growth rate of ×20 per gigasecond, and can achieve growth rates as high as ×27 per gigasecond if all the resources are poured into further expanding its power and production infrastructure. These growth rates were determined experimentally on 26-03-2026 by playing the finespun economic simulator (which is currently still work in progress) for 1 gigasecond of ingame time

Name and flag

The settlement was named Finespun after the loose composition of the asteroid that centers it, and the architecture that would be built within it in the form of a vast web of tunnels, which ended up never actually being built.

The flag of finespun is an abstract depiction of a kamacite crystal, showing off the mineral's 3-way symmetry, chosen for the symbol due to kamacite being the primary building material for nearly all of Finespun infrastructure, by far the most produced material locally, and also a key element of the local technology.

Finespun measures growth primarily in power generation, since with the local tech tree it translates very well to nearly everything else. Specific targets had been set, assuming a 20× growth in power generation each gigasecond. After an initial boost from trading and altruistic agents to reach the kardashev-zero level, the settlement is set to be fully self-sufficient and self-growing. During the first gigasecond, the growth rate is maximized, reaching 27× per gigasecond, but a planned setback knocked it down to the expected 20× by the 16th gigasecond.

Real power generation stays ahead of the target power generation to account for various setbacks. If power generation falls behind schedule, resources are reassigned from less important activities to building out the power and production infrastructure, which at maximum can speed up growth to 27× per gigasecond. The settlement is scheduled to complete its growth around epoch time 18 844 000 000 - if no further needs arise, this would be the settlement's final size.

Increasing power production beyond 150 gigawatts is unlikely - one solar array covering the entirety of Finespun's perihelion hill sphere would produce about 150GW at apohelion, and the real scenario planned for is 400 000 smaller arrays covering some significant fraction of that area. More power could be achieved by moving outside the hill sphere of the Finespun asteroid but that is considered expansion rather than growth of the settlement itself. The computational grid of the settlement is unlikely to expand beyond the hill sphere for latency reasons - 200 microseconds of light lag across the hill sphere's diameter already potentially limits the minds running on the grid to no more than 5kHz inference rate with unlucky computronium allocation, and the administrator's mind is very big.

Around epoch time 15 440 000 000 the settlement reproduced by budding to the nearby asteroid 36735, with which a close encounter occurred for several megaseconds right around this time. This involved moving a full set of initial infrastructure including 4 functional solar power arrays to the new asteroid, as well as several instances of UEIA and a highly overclocked copy of administrator Felicity - this created a second settlement whose growth timeline is identical aside from being offset by about 500 megaseconds into the future. Giving away 4 solar arrays at that stage erased about 90 megaseconds of work from the settlement's growth timeline and caused it to nearly miss the 16-gigasecond target of 80 solar arrays. After the 16th gigasecond, the settlement's resource use was able to be relaxed and maintained the 20× per-gigasecond growth rate with a small amount of resources left over for side-activities such as habitat construction and improvement.

timekeeping

The local standard of timekeeping is Epoch Time, and the chosen cycle length is 90 kiloseconds, exactly equal to 25 hours, which was chosen because the lack of an overt day-night cycle combined with the availability of instant on-demand artificial lighting tends to delay the onset of sleep of the occasional human visitors, extending their circadian cycle to roughly that length.

Dates are written with digits spaced in groups of three, and the time of day is simply calculated as the epoch time modulo cycle length, which at the moment would correspond to [time]. The megasecond stands in for the local equivalent of a week and/or month. No equivalent of noon or midnight is set - the settlement as a whole maintains a fairly consistent level of activity throughout the whole cycle, with local time used mainly just for synchronization rather than specifically corresponding to parts of a day-night cycle.

160278 Finespun is an S-type asteroid belonging to the Tirela family of asteroids. It has enough mass to produce its own hill sphere, allowing it to have its own orbital infrastructure up to about 30km away from its surface.

The matter of fact standard for where to place orbiting objects around the asteroids is an altitude of 10km from the asteroid's center of mass, where one orbit takes about 16 552 seconds and the orbital velocity is around 3.796m/s.

The asteroid does not have enough surface gravity to walk on, and surface movement has to be limited to less than 5m/s or else risk entering an orbit. This fact is frequently exploited by the mining workers who simply manually throw pods full of regolith onto the a trajectory intersecting the orbital station that will take it in for refining. Cold gas thrusters using waste oxygen from regolith refining tend to be largely sufficient for most purposes given the small orbital velocities.

Nearby objects of interest

160278 Finespun experiences close encounters with asteroid number 36735 every 290 terran years ≈ 9.15Gs. The close encounters within this millennium happen specifically in the gregorian years 2169, 2459, 2749 and one in 3039 earliest next millennium. At the closest their distance from each other is about 0.04 AU ≈ 19.96 light seconds ≈ 5.984E+9 metres.

Culture

Environmental design

Safety-relevant equipment is often placed even where it is not needed just in case that it might be needed someday. Useful infrastructure is sometimes built in seemingly random places just for the sake of there being useful infrastructure. By administrator Felicity's guidance, wherever the people of Finespun stay, there should be useful infrastructure left behind, especially infrastructure that could be useful in an emergency, for the sake of anyone who may end up in the same places in the future. This rule is applied even beyond the Finespun territory, such as when conducting mining expeditions to other asteroids, as such many of the nearby space objects are littered with obvious signs that someone had been there. Occasionally the more extensive examples of such abandoned infrastructure may include AI that decided to remain active within it to perform minimal maintenance, observe the society from a distance and await anyone who may visit in need of help.

Local standards

The adopted audible environmental alert consists of a sinewave tone held constant at 500Hz for 0.25 seconds, then raised in frequency by one whole octave (up to 1000Hz) across 0.5 seconds, and then held constant at 1000Hz for 0.75 seconds, followed by 3 seconds of silence, repeated as long as the environmental emergency is present. Task assignment uses a particular format shown below. This is used for both workers and visitors, a core of this system being the idea that as thinking beings, the recipients of such task specification cards will be able to inference implicit details based on being told why something is being done and the context around it, which would be very hard to explain explicitly.
Task Reason
  • precise description of the task
  • where to go
  • what to do
  • when to do it
  • how to do it

    • Specifying tasks is the most basic element of every plan
  • bigger picture
  • why your task is important
  • what will happen to others if you succeed
  • what will happen to others if you fail

    • Explaining why something is being done has a tendency to convey a lot of important information about how it should be done and what to do in case of unexpected events or circumstances
Coordination Context
  • who to report to with success, failure, progress or problems
  • how to maintain contact
  • what to do if you cannot make contact

    • Communication about tasks has an excessively large effect on the overall quality of work and ensuring it can happen is critical enough and hard enough to deserve its own field. Others must know what you're going to do even when they cannot see or contact you
  • the basics and the obvious
  • who you are working for
  • who you are and why you've been selected
  • free card for any other information you want to provide
  • even if something sounds too obvious, just write enough here to fill the field

    • Intelligent agents tend to organize randomly, and often end up with strange gaps in their knowledge and especially can miss information that is so obvious that nobody bothers to mention it explicitly, so providing even random semi-relevant scraps of information will stop these gaps from accumulating to the point of failure
