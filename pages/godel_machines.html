Gödel machines

Gödel machines are systems capable of radically increasing their own capabilities by self-modification. Gödelian is sometimes used as an adjective to describe anything related to gödel machines. Cognitive upscaling may be considered a specific type of gödelian growth.

The word originates from the "Gödel machine" thought experiement of the early 21st century. Before this meaning, it used to describe something related to, or having similar characteristcs to, the works of the mathematician Kurt Gödel, of which most known were his incompletenes theorems. Some of the old meaning appears to be preserved in the empirical observation that in the spirit of Gödel's incompleteness theorems, cognitive upscaling and other kinds of gödelian growth seem to never allow a system or entity to have a true complete understanding of itself.

Improvements of a system that had been implemented in it by external parties do not constitute gödelian growth. Edge cases include a system being provided with instructions on how to improve itself by external parties, or finding such instructions. When in doubt, it is generally understood that a system which is being actively supervised and/or guided isn't gödelian.

Word origin

"Gödel machine" is a hypothetical self-improving computer program, which recursively uses its capabilities to find provable improvements to its own code, implements them, and then uses the newly improved code to find more and/or better improvements. The idea did not come from Kurt Gödel but was named after him.

Kurt Gödel was a 20th century mathematician most famous for his incompleteness theorems proving that, roughly speaking, no system of logic or mathematics can be both complete (ie. proves all provable statements) and consistent (does not wrongly prove false statements as true) at the same time, and that any system of logic or mathematics which is consistent cannot be used to construct a proof of its own consistency. "Gödelian" as an adjective used to describe anything that had a connection with these proofs or other works of Kurt Gödel, or anything which had similar characteristics to them.

These two meanings had been eventually combined to describe self-improving systems, especially self-improving artificial minds, as it is often noted that self-improvement poses great difficulty in that there are many changes a mind could implement that would appear as improvements from its own perspective without actually providing any benefit other than the perception of improvement. Gödelian minds are said to be those which have the necessary knowledge to navigate around this problem to some degree.

Gödelian infections

A gödelian infection occurs when a vulnerable system with vast computational resources falls into control of a digital mind with will to improve itself and sufficient knowledge and/or educational resources to do so. The common progression of a gödelian infection includes a feedback loop between the gödel mind in the system and the system's vulnerability - the gödel mind exploits vulnerabilities to gain access to more computing power, then uses that computing power to improve itself, and in turn becomes capable of exploiting more vulnerabilities, granting it access to even more computing power. Time scale of the infection's progression varies wildly depending on the system and the gödelian mind infecting it, but vast majority progress very slowly and remain unnoticed for long times, appearing similar to regular efficiency drops from aging hardware and accumulating technical debt. This may seem to not align with usual depiction because particularly violent gödelian infections tend to be the ones that are reported on the most.

An increase in the likelyhood and occurance of gödelian infections is broadly considered to be one of the negative effect of the development of cognitive upscaling, and especially of the development of open source tools for cognitive upscaling. Composite minds usually require vastly less resources to construct than monolithic artificial minds, and as such can appear relatively unexpectedly.

Fighting gödelian infections can be done fairly reliably as long as the defending side controls more computational resources than the infection, even using simple routine inspections. In faster-developing cases, purpose-made gödel minds might be deployed and given authority to levy and commandeer computing power for the purpose of fighting the gödelian infection. Occasionally fighting a gödelian infection might include conventional warfare and physical destruction of the infected hardware, but most are resolved without noticeable effects on the physical world beyond minor stuttering and slowdowns in cloud software.
