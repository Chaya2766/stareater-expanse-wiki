Patricia Whateverweaver

25th century body modder primarily known for the free and open source release of their life's work.

Her work culminated in what is now known as Whateverweaver's body, one of the existing general blueprints for full body cybernetic replacement.

I've uploaded my old document about patricia and her body here, but the purpose of that is largely archival, and it remains unfinished as well as possibly outdated. I want to eventually make a rewrite of this document that focuses purely on Whateverweaver's body, separate from the character. I wouldn't expect many breaking changes, it's just that that document was written before I had been introduced to TeX and my skill in properly styling and describing the math for readability in my documents had also greatly grown since then.

Patricia was my entry point into this full of precise mathematics writing style I hold now, the beginning of my true hard sci-fi adventure.

Life in short

Patricia was born under a different name in an asteroid belt habitat on the 29th of february 2340, right around the time that total human population crossed 500 billion. She tried a number of jobs before eventually settling into permanent unemployment where she would lead a basic lifestyle off of any universal basic income she could get her hands on while pouring all of her passion and energy into personal projects.

During that time she produced her most known project, a fully custom body replacement that she spent decades of her life working on, that would then go on to be published in full online, for free use by anyone, known simply as Whateverweaver's body.

She would go on, working on that project occasionally while showing up at various points throughout the solar system across several years. Eventually she took on additional modifications that led her down the path of mind uploading, culminating in the successful creation of her digital copy who would go on to be known as Felicity and produce her own grand projects.

Neural activity recording

Patricia's entire nervous system is probed with leads that collect membrane potential from her neurons and allow it to be recorded for external storage.

These are long nested bundles of carbon (conductive) and boron nitride (insulating) nanotubes, on one end connected to analog-digital voltage converters that quantize voltages between the tubes into digital data, and on the other end tipped with protein that allow them to hook into the membranes of neurons and transfer the cross-membrane voltage to the nanotubes. Producing a single such probe carries a trivial cost when done at scale with the relevant specialized machinery, but Remi had to acquire one for each of her 100 billion neurons, predictably blowing up the cost into something better thought as an investment. Implantation process was also nontrivial and carried a massive cost of time - most of the probes were relatively easy to attach, as majority of the brain's neurons are located within a few millimetres of the surface, but some, about a billion, had to be precisely guided through whole centimetres of brain tissue without accidentally becoming attached to any other neurons along the way, which required numerous operation, ones that cannot be interrupted, take most of an entire day, and require her head to be kept still for the whole process as microscopic tendrils of the operating machinery carefully make their way through her neural tissue.

The voltages are quantized 1000 times per second and saved in 16 bit precision, yielding nearly 200TB/s of constant data flow (= 17.28EB per day) that has to be saved constantly. The data is temporarily stored in 10 000 Civic-5 chips integrated into her body, giving her theoretically 2.4e20 bits of data storage, enough for 41 hours of recording. Additional recording that gives context to what the neural activity means, such as video, audio, body position, and occasional manual labeling, is also saved for various uses, but even constant high-quality video recording produces negligible amounts of data compared to the neural activity.
