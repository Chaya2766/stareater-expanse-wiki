Patricia was born at approximately 13 448 388 860 epoch time (29th of february 2396), just a few years after total living human population passed 1 trillion. Her place of birth was an asteroid belt rotating habitat, relatively poor by the standards of the time. She never truly had to work and simply settled into permanent unemployment where she would lead a basic lifestyle off of any universal basic income she could get her hands on while pouring all of her passion and energy into personal projects.

During that time she produced her most known (relatively speaking at least) project, a fully custom body replacement that she spent decades of her life working on, and left published in full online, for free use by anyone, known simply as Whateverweaver's Body. This project also largely defined who she was in her own view and refined her passions.

Another project which she started in paralel continued the theme of producing deeply documented free technology, seeking to construct a full ecosystem of tools that would be sufficient to support a space settlement while also being simple enough that a single person could understand it down to each component involved. Having two such massive projects in paralel was such an overwhelming workload that it occasionally crossed from actual engineering into mere highly technical storywriting - a character by the name of Felicity was created in this way, and gradually became sophisticated enough for Patricia to potentially take on as a new identity. This culminated in her receiving material support that funded her mind uploading as a part of the project, resulting in the successful creation of her digital copy - Felicity thus became a real person as Patricia's digital copy, and took over the project, with the existing sohpisticated identity of a fictional character being available for her making the post-uploading transition and identity shock much easier to handle.

Patricia continued the work on Whateverweaver's Body for about 32 years after it became her actual body, with the public repository continuing to receive updates as part of that work. During that time the project underwent a level of scope creep that became unacceptable, in result Patricia spend the end of that time simplifying and contracting it back to original intent, and refining the solution she ended up with, eventually finalizing it and continuing the removed additions as separate projects.

After the completion of the project she took a long break from doing anything productive, but afterwards began to capitalize on her slowly growing popularity in the overlap between the furry and body modder communities, continuing her hobby as a body designer with somewhat less attention to detail and less pressure on doing everything herself, but still just as much adherence to freely sharing the results of her work for the benefit of general public.

Reimplementation in new brain tissue culture

The publication given title of "Reimplementation of a volunteer human mind in new brain tissue culture" was the peak of BSc. Patricia Whateverweaver's academic career, and the closest she had gotten to making a truly groundbreaking contribution to humanity's scientific progress. Uncharacteristically for the standards of the time it did not list hundreds of authors, but just eight.

It did not take long for minor controversy to arise with rumors that BSc. Patricia Whateverweaver, as opposed to the doctors and PhDs making up the rest of the team, may have been pressured into providing her neurological data for the experiment, but she publicly refuted such claims, countering that actually she was given a perhaps inappropriately large amount of power over the entire project specifically in caution to avoid any possibility of that happening. Technically the project was a form of live human testing, in form that legally many jurisdictions do not even have volunteer exceptions for, and avoids including actual murder largely just through a technicality that may not hold in some jurisdictions either. Under Noca Bell doctrine however, this was permitted, even if allegedly only through legal trickery provided by the very composite mind who wrote the law.

Contrary to the common misreporting, this was not nearly as much a breakthrough as it is portrayed to be, as it bases on thousands of arguably even more impressive publications such as "Induction of single synapse connections in live human neurons", and did not actually solve all the issues of re-implementing a human mind in a new neuron culture, it merely progressed the field in the direction that collectively was very clear by providing an actual volunteer who had both the will to make themselves the test subject of such a procedure and the necessary data and implants already available, and precisely documenting the entire procedure and observed results. Although it merely moved one narrow category of one field of science one step forward, Patricia is hugely proud of having given a small and incremental but no less real gain to humanity's overall technological progress.

Patricia Whateverweaver made one public statement when asked about it in public by surprise.
"In the end, where we're at now, is one step closer to living in a science fiction novel where you can just load your mind into a new body after you die, sure it's a complicated process right now, but imagine how much cheaper it could be than post-mortem digital reconstruction once it is refined and optimized. I hope that we can um, in the future nobody is forced to just accept permanent death because they can't afford digital reconstruction, or something. Okay let me just take this opportunity to say, I was the project lead, nobody forced me to do it, but there should be a lot more law around how you treat data that can be used to completely reconstruct a person's mind, it's um . . yeah, uploads had been talking about it for gigka ae gigaseconds and it still looks uhh, way too much like it's being treated as just ae, a hypothetical possibility."
She largely disappeared from public view afterwards for nearly a 200 megaseconds (during which she would stay primarily at the Finespun mining outpost) due to stress over the public response, especially as she did not expect the study for which she provided herself as a test subject to get any significant attention beyond purely academic circles. The minor local media storm surrounding her had dissipated completely within that time but by then it had already dealt a minor bit of acute psychological damage to her, presenting in the form of persecution syndrome, which as visible symptoms included identity-hiding practices in social interactions and excessive attention to privacy (to a degree which disrupts everyday life).

Main body

Patricia's usual body is a fully inorganic body replacement that she spent over a gigasecond of her life developing, and released freely for public benefit. It is her favourite and by far the most thoroughly thought out one of her bodies.

Neural activity recording

Patricia's entire nervous system is probed with leads that collect membrane potential from her neurons and allow it to be recorded for external storage.

These are long nested bundles of carbon (conductive) and boron nitride (insulating) nanotubes, on one end connected to analog-digital voltage converters that quantize voltages between the tubes into digital data, and on the other end tipped with protein that allow them to hook into the membranes of neurons and transfer the cross-membrane voltage to the nanotubes. Producing a single such probe carries a trivial cost when done at scale with the relevant specialized machinery, but Remi had to acquire one for each of her 100 billion neurons, predictably blowing up the cost into something better thought as an investment. Implantation process was also nontrivial and carried a massive cost of time - most of the probes were relatively easy to attach, as majority of the brain's neurons are located within a few millimetres of the surface, but some, about a billion, had to be precisely guided through whole centimetres of brain tissue without accidentally becoming attached to any other neurons along the way, which required numerous operation, ones that cannot be interrupted, take most of an entire day, and require her head to be kept still for the whole process as microscopic tendrils of the operating machinery carefully make their way through her neural tissue.

The voltages are quantized 1000 times per second and saved in 16 bit precision, yielding nearly 200TB/s of constant data flow (= 17.28EB per day) that has to be saved constantly. The data is temporarily stored in 10 000 Civic-5 chips integrated into her tail, giving her theoretically 2.4e20 bits of data storage, enough for 150ks (41 hours) of recording. Additional recording that gives context to what the neural activity means, such as video, audio, body position, and occasional manual labeling, is also saved for various uses, but even constant high-quality video recording produces negligible amounts of data compared to the neural activity.

The civic-5 chips used initially were merely an in-development prototype that patricia volunteered to test, and in that way made a small but constant contribution to their further development by gathering daily diagnostics and performance data. The amount of data being constantly saved to the chips dissipates 128 watts of power which makes her tail noticeably warmer than the rest of her body.

Body hopping

After finishing the development of her main body, Patricia continued to develop new bodies for herself and sharing them, as well as using existing designs shared freely among body modder communities that happened to satisfy her standards of documentation and reproducibility. Among other things, it serves her as a form of brain exercise, as every body switch forces her to almost completely relearn most motor skills.

The bodies tend to be named after some of the fictional characters that Patricia used in collaborative writing with her friends. The desire to be truly free to change bodies at will, including all the vital organs and even the brain, pushed her to eventually present herself as a test subject for research on reimplementation of existing human minds in cultured brain tissue.

It had been noted that leading up to her role in resurrection research, she had increasingly taken on a disturbingly optimistic attitude towards death.

"Vele"

A colorful, aquatic-styled body made entirely with biotech, and rather full of strange choices due to being intended more for aesthetic than practical use. 5 antenae grow out of the head, equipped with enlarged tips that glow brightly. The yellow color of the eyes prevents much blue light from getting in, which causes quite severe difficulty in telling apart colours that to a human eye would appear as yellow and white, or blue and black, effectively the same as tritanopia.

The most unique aspect of the Vele body is that it is equipped with a "capacitor organ". It uses specially designed tissue whose cells can synthesize hydrogen peroxide and store it in internal vacuoles when the blood is sufficiently oxygenated, as well as absorb carbon dioxide from blood and store it as a concentrated solution of carbonic acid. When the blood is not sufficiently oxygenated, hydrogen peroxide stored in the cells is broken down for oxygen, meanwhile when the blood does have high concentrations of oxygen it is used to refill the hydrogen peroxide stockpiles. Similarly when the blood has excess carbon dioxide concentration the cells will use it to synthesize and store carbonic acid, and break it down slowly to empty the storage vacuoles when the carbon dioxide concentration is low.

Notably both hydrogen peroxide and carbonic acid will readily break down and produce gasses if somehow allowed to leak out from the correct celular structures, which means that the organ will begin outgasing and bubbling noticeably when damaged. For this reason it is equipped with its own separate circulatory system, which only interacts with the main circulatory system by osmosis, but sufficient damage could still lead to a gas embolism.
