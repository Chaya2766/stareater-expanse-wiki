Artificial Minds

Artificial Minds are systems constructed by engineering, capable of performing complex tasks that historically required people.

The rate of development of artificial minds had historically stuck closely to the overall rate of resource acquisition by humanity. AM capabilities progressed smoothly across 1000 years since the 21st century, at first only being useful in supporting roles, then gradually nearing human capability, then matching it, and after a long time surpassing it, at first by just a little, but gradually eventually growing to such an extent that unmodified human experts cannot adequately understand the capabilities of the most capable models.

To this day, vast majority of artificial minds are simple cyclic neural networks.

The exact timeline of expert systems, AGI and ASI development that I portray is based on this simple estimate. The claims of how capable models of each given size would be are my arbitrary decisions.
As to why I chose to say "artificial mind" instead of "artificial intelligence", I see it as more fitting since I expect vast majority of artificial minds in the setting to not actually have much intelligence, but rather simply have a pre-existing static set of knowledge and behaviours that are already useful enough to make intelligence unnecessary.

Subcategories and vocabulary

For general purpose use:
  1. Expert systems - programs which can supply similar benefits as having access to an expert in some field of knowledge
  2. Agents - programs that are capable of taking action on their own initiative, usually to seek completion of a goal they are given
  3. Intelligence - the ability of a program to acquire new skills and abilities during operation. Also used to refer to programs or instances of programs which display this ability.
  4. Intellect - somewhat obscure way of referring to one exact instance of a program which displays general intelligence
  5. Embodied - being put in control of a body that allows interactions with the physical world


Function-centric classification models and networks:
  1. Navigator - model whose primary capability is making decisions based on available information
  2. Synthesizer - model whose primary capability is creation of complex data such as text, images, sound, voxel maps, node graphs and similar
  3. Analyzer - model whose primary capability is absorbing complex data and returning conclusions (eg. image classifiers)
  4. Translator - model whose primary capability is converting complex data between representations (eg. brain-to-brain interface networks)
  5. Reservior - model whose primary capability is selectively storing and preserving received information for later retrieval
This classification is most useful in the context of cognitive upscaling where very often multiple originally separate networks are brought together in various ways to become one system. It is common for the same model to be classified differently based on context, and models may be rated on how advanced their capabilities as a navigator or analyzer or any of the others are, as the more complex networks are likely to have at least some capacity in all categories.

The term "Artificial Minds" is generally more accurate than its nearly adjcent predecessor "Artificial Intelligence" in that it does not imply intelligence, which is quite low if present at all in most modern artificial minds, but it still carries a little controversy as depending on interpretation it may suggest capacity for subjective experiences, which some people do not agree is necessarily present in these systems.

Architecture

Most artificial minds are simple cyclic neural networks, similar to the earlier deep neural networks but arranged in a cyclic graph instead of layers. This architecture naturally lends itself well to continuous operation as information circulates around inside the network instead of being recalculated completely at every iteration. An iteration typically refers to calculating the next neuron activation of every neuron from their current activations - other terms for this include inference and simulation frame.

Cognitive capabilities as a function of neural network size

Raw end-to-end neural networks gain the ability to display certain capabilities after reaching a specific size, measured as the number of parameters, ie. the connections between neurons within the network. This depends heavily on how the network was trained and certain implementational details, so the pessimistic and optimistic cases may begin to display them about an order of magnitude earlier or later than expected. The common pattern is that after the first time a model exists that is capable of something, sometime soon another model is made that is roughly 10 times smaller and still capable of it.

Ofcourse radically different architectures that do not rely on neurons give completely different results, so the points below are to be understood as only applicable to end to end stateful neural networks. This excludes human brains modeled and implemented as computer simulations. For those who are interested in specifics of measuring cognitive abilities directly rather than loosely estimating them based on network size, the psychometrics page will be a better fit than the list below.

100 billion - Around this size models begin to gain understanding of human languages at the level of underlying meaning.

1 trillion - Sufficient for expert systems that analyze problems in nearly any scientific domain and recall known solutions.

10 trillion - Ability to synthesize new knowledge that did not exist in the training dataset from the built in memorized knowledge, hints of general intelligence.

100 trillion - Noticeable ability to synthesize knowledge from received information even if it does not closely relate to any existing knowledge in the model.

1 quadrillion to 100 quadrillion - Ability to learn new knowledge and skills within the human range.
1 quadrillion parameters is notably close to the 1 quadrillion synapses in the human brain.
Models in this range are most noted for cases of "goofy and unhinged behaviour" as a result of their closeness to human level of cognitive abilities.

1 quintillion - Ability to acquire new knowledge and skills past the human range. Ability to construct novel mental models to explain observations surpassing the most skilled pre-AI human thinkers.

1 sextillion - Commonly display ability to adjust fluidly enough and build new mental models so proficiently as to exceed performance of experienced human experts even when faced with small samples of new unexpected information.

1 septillion - [not decided yet]

Composite minds

More powerful artificial minds can be made by combining multiple existing artificial minds into one system, but such combinations are usually less effective per the number of parameters than equivalently sized complete neural networks trained as one part. This observation is often suggested as an explanation for the disproportionately massive size of human brain emulations compared to AI models with humanlike cognitive capabilities.

Becoming a composite mind is one of the pathways that a digitized mind can take to become significantly more mentally capable, see cognitive upscaling for more information.

writing notes

My writing of generalist artificial minds assumes a completely arbitrary relationship between active memory and parameter count according to this exact formula:

active_memory = sqrt(parameter_count)/8e6

This is not grounded in reality at all, but it plays well with composite minds, in that they have nice tradeoffs. The relation between computational requirements and active memory for composite minds is O(n^4) but for monolithic neural networks only O(n^2), but maximum model size is pinned to the timeline whereas composite minds just need more computation so you could get far ahead if you are just willing to spend gigawatts running it.

So in conclusion I am sticking to this just because this is better than nothing. I'm going to selectively apply this, treating it as a sort of soft upper bound on the capabilities of generalist AI models, until I have a better idea.
