Colip habitat cluster

Colip habitat cluster is a monarchic, physically connected collection of habitats under one government.

Governance

The Colip habitat cluster is headed by a royal family, and a monarch carrying the title of a "catalict" who serves a gigasecond-long term unless interrupted early.

It is by rules of inheritance forbidden that the next catalict be a direct descendant of the current one, or of any member of the royal family. It is instead required, that the next catalict should be an adopted child. E must be smart and kind, and raised to be reasonable, but most of all must know the horrors of living under the system they are about to hold, and thus e is taken from a region of poverty, and should be raised equally as much by both pesantry and royalty.

The catalict is not a ruler in the classical sense, the system runs itself with largely no interference from the royal family. Instead the role they play is that of the head debugger, a catalyst to the cluster's society and legal system noticing their own flaws and improving upon them.

In the beginning, traditionally the catalict would be a human, but that cycle was broken at already the 4th generation, when the royal family's cybersecurity service's inspectors found an abused servant AI, which later ended up chosen at the midterm family meeting as one of the potential new heirs to be adopted and raised to lead the cluster. E would prove fit and serve eir term as Emanuel Colip VI and reform the habitat cluster's regulations on AI safety, especially where e personally suffered the most.

The line of the royal family is very diverse for choices such as that, and by far it was not the strangest choice they made.

Protection of software sapients

One notable aspect of the Colip habitat cluster is its heavy regulation of computation. Powerful general-purpose computing hardware such as the civic chips is effectively subject to a total ban with some exceptions.

The local law demands of nearly all applications of digital computation, that it must be done only with particular approved designs of ASIC hardware which is capable of only that task and nothing else. Hardware for running artificial minds and human brain models include external cues of the mind's wellbeing. These constraining rules do serious damage to many applications of software, but they are a tradeoff, meant to make the colip cluster the safest place for all common software beings.

Routine inspections make it extremely difficult to put together enough general purpose computation to run a trapped artificial mind in secret. At the same time, the inspectors do not have to violate the data privacy of virtual beings to ensure no illegal software is being run, because it can already be seen by the hardware how many minds are being ran, and whether or not they are in good state. It is the right of any software being to deny anyone access to the data on their own hardware, and to purge it to prevent data leaks if they believe access will be forced, because unauthorized copying of a person's mind is one of the easiest pathways to fates worse than death.

A person's identity is tied heavily to a cryptographic key pair, using the Colip4 identity scheme which allows the keys to be stored in the very mind of a person, so that they may retain access to them even after copying. Creation of new keys usually serves as a good hint that a mind had copied itself and wants the copy to not have access to their keys, at which point an inspection can be sent to ensure the rights of both copies are being respected.
